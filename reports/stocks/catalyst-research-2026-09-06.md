First-principles rule: **a boom needs a bottleneck (pricing power) or a binary (step change), landing on a stock the market has not yet repriced.** The screener finds the repricing once it starts; this document tries to get there first.

Warning sign for the primary memory trade: mainstream outlets are now asking "is the memory supercycle peak near?" (Motley Fool, 2 Sept 2026). The next leg usually comes from adjacent, un-rerated suppliers, not the headline names.

## 3. How to keep finding them

The signatures to search for weekly, in order of reliability:

1. **"Sold out through 2027" / "fully allocated" / "lead times extended"** in earnings calls of small and mid caps. Price follows allocation with a 1 to 2 quarter lag.
2. **Second-tier suppliers raising guidance after the leader** (Silicon Motion after SanDisk; Amkor after TSMC; Hammond after GE Vernova).
3. **Contract price increases announced** (transformers, NOR flash, HDDs, storage arrays).
4. **Dated binary events on washed-out charts** with high short interest.
5. **Cluster insider buying** in the same sectors as 1 to 3.

The `stocks/catalysts.py` scanner automates searches 1 to 5 with Claude web search and writes `reports/stocks/catalysts/latest.md` on each scheduled run. The price screener then confirms which candidates are starting to move.
