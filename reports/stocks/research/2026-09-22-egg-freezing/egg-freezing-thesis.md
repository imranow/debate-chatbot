## 1. The answer first

The thesis has the direction right and the timing wrong. Three facts matter most.

1. **The demographic premise is already true and has largely stopped moving.** Unmarried US women aged 15 and over slightly outnumber married women (49.9% married, per the Population Reference Bureau; the data year is probably 2009-10 and is unconfirmed). Among all US adults, Pew shows the trend stalling: unpartnered adults fell from 44% (2019) to 42% (2023), and married adults ticked up to 51%. Marriages rebounded in South Korea (+14.8% in 2024, +8.1% in 2025) and China (+10.8% in 2025). The real driver is later childbearing, not marital status.
2. **The market is small and growth appears to have stalled.** US egg freezing grew from about 6,100 cycles in 2014 to about 38,000 in 2023, roughly 23% a year. SART's preliminary 2024 report, as seen in one search snippet we could not check against the SART site, shows 38,930 cycles. That is about 2% more than SART's final 2023 figure of 38,126, and about 1% less than the 39,269 some sources give for 2023. Either way, growth appears to have stalled, pending SART's final 2024 data. UK volumes were flat in 2024. At roughly $16,000 a cycle, US egg freezing is about $620M of retrieval spend a year (2024 volumes; storage adds roughly $60M by our estimate). About two thirds of it (the 2021 elective share) is elective, or about $415M.
3. **There is almost no listed pure play.** The companies that capture most of the money (US clinic chains, TMRW/Reprotech storage, Ferring, Maven, Carrot, Kindbody) are private. Among listed names, only Progyny (about 5 to 11% of revenue, not disclosed) and Kitazato (about 3 to 9%, probably an upper bound) plausibly have more than 5% of revenue from elective egg freezing. For Merck KGaA, Cooper, Vitrolife and Organon it is under 1%.

**The best-quality listed exposure is Kitazato (368A.T)**, the Japanese maker of the Cryotop vitrification kit used to freeze eggs and embryos. It is a real chokepoint outside the US. About 65% of its sales come from vitrification. It has a 70% share in Europe, 80% in China and 85% in India, according to an investor write-up rather than the company; its US share is not known. It has an operating margin of about 54% and trades at about 13 to 14 times earnings (aggregator data). The case for owning it is as a bet on all frozen-cycle IVF, not on egg freezing alone. Risks: it is a small cap; returns are exposed to the yen; it depends on one new US distributor; and automated vitrification (Genea Gavi) or in-vitro maturation could change lab workflow. China, where its share is highest, bans elective egg freezing for single women.

## 2. First principles: where the money in one egg-freezing journey goes

A US woman who freezes her eggs at 34 and pays herself spends roughly $33,500 over ten years in the base case.

| Stream | When | Expected spend | Who captures it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Egg retrieval (about 1.5 cycles at $16,000) | Year 0 | $24,000 (72%) | Clinic about $9,000 to $11,000 per cycle (sources differ); drug makers $2,500 to $4,500; lab consumables $300 to $800 inside the clinic fee |
| Storage ($500 to $1,000 a year) | Years 1 to 9 | $5,300 (16%) | Clinic or offsite storage company; near-zero marginal cost |
| Thaw, fertilisation and transfer (assumes 20% return over 10 years, above the 6 to 9% seen in US studies so far) | Years 4 to 8 | $2,400 (7%) | Clinic, IVF-stage labs, genetic testing |
| Fresh IVF after a failed thaw | Years 5 to 10 | $1,800 (5%) | Clinic, drug makers |

Assumptions: 1.5 cycles, $750 a year storage with 95% annual retention, 20% return, 40% thaw failure. FertilityIQ reports an average of 2 cycles.

Three implications follow. The clinic keeps most of the money. Storage is an attractive annuity, because specimens rarely move once frozen. It is small, though: roughly $60M a year of US storage revenue on our estimate, and cheaper offsite storage is a competitive threat. And the later IVF cycle, often described as the big prize, is a small part of expected value. Only 6% (US, after 5 to 7 years) to 28% (Australia and New Zealand, after 10 or more years) of women have come back for their eggs.

## 4. Why the bull case struggles (devil's-advocate findings)

- **Volume growth stopped in 2024** in the two best-measured markets, the US (about +2% on SART's final 2023 base, from a single preliminary snippet) and the UK (flat). Total US ART cycles were 431,746 in SART's preliminary 2024 report, 1.4% above the final 2023 figure of 425,869 (other sources give 415,953 to about 432,000 for 2023). That came even as the fertility rate hit a record low of 1.57 in 2025.
- **Prices are falling across the chain.** TrumpRx cash prices for the three-drug Merck regimen are up to 84% below list (company claim). Organon fertility fell 9% excluding currency in Q1 2026. Low-cost clinics charge from $6,000 before drugs (Kindbody) to $7,600 all-in (CNY Fertility). Cofertility freezes for free if half the eggs are donated. Offsite storage at $200 to $500 a year undercuts clinic storage at $500 to $1,000.
- **Policy helps medical infertility, not elective freezing.** The proposed federal "excepted fertility benefits" rule (May 2026) appears to cover medical infertility only (rule text not checked). California's SB 729 also targets medical infertility. Employer-paid elective freezing is taxable income: about $4,400 of extra federal income tax on a $20,000 benefit for a woman earning $80,000 (CNBC; payroll and state taxes would add more). Employer egg-freezing coverage rose only from 16% (2024) to 18% in 2026 (IFEBP, via a secondary source; Mercer puts large-employer coverage at 21%).
- **The track record is poor.** Kindbody's valuation has reportedly fallen from $1.8bn to a targeted $600M, and possibly as low as about $400M (undated reports). Progyny trades at about $27, well below its 2021 peak (reported at about $60+, unverified). Monash IVF collapsed after clinical errors. Private-equity clinic multiples compressed from 11 to 15 times EBITDA (operating profit before depreciation and amortisation) to 6 to 14 times.
- **Technology could cut drug use per cycle.** Gameto says its Fertilo shortens hormone stimulation from about two weeks to 2 to 3 days; its US Phase 3 interim readout is due in late 2026 (company guidance). Growing eggs from stem cells is at least a decade away.

What the bull case gets right: age-related infertility will keep rising as first births move later (US mean age 27.5, South Korea about 33); egg freezing did grow about 23% a year from 2014 to 2023; storage is a sticky annuity; Kitazato is a genuine chokepoint; and subsidies in Tokyo, Seoul and Taiwan show governments leaning in.

## 5. How to capitalise, honestly

- **Look at the chokepoint, not the narrative.** Kitazato has a dominant, high-margin position in freezing itself outside the US. It benefits from all frozen cycles (egg and embryo), which are a large and rising share of IVF cycles (17% of UK cycles in 2024).
- **Wait for Progyny to get cheap, and treat it as a fertility-benefits business, not an egg-freezing play.** It has the largest estimated elective exposure of any listed name (about 5 to 11%, undisclosed); Kitazato's is about 3 to 9%. Its egg-freezing share is undisclosed, its use per member is falling, and the loss of Amazon to Maven (effective Jan 2025) argues for buying only well below today's ~$27 (52-week low $16.10).
- **Recognise that the best economics are private.** Clinic chains with owned storage (US Fertility, IVI-RMA) and storage consolidators (TMRW/Reprotech) capture the annuity. Public investors can only reach them through IPOs, and the most prominent one in the pipeline (Indira IVF, India) is a full exit by EQT and the founders.
- **Think about the wider thesis.** If the real driver is later childbearing, the broader beneficiaries are IVF volume and frozen-cycle labs, not elective egg freezing. That points back to Kitazato and, on a large drawdown, Vitrolife.
- **Avoid the drug makers for this thesis.** Egg freezing is under 1% of their revenue and their US prices are being cut.

## Glossary

- **ART**: assisted reproductive technology (IVF, egg freezing and related procedures).
- **SART**: Society for Assisted Reproductive Technology, which publishes US clinic data. **HFEA**: the UK fertility regulator.
- **Vitrification**: flash-freezing eggs or embryos so ice crystals do not form.
- **EBITDA**: operating profit before depreciation and amortisation. **P/E**: share price divided by earnings per share.
- **Organic growth**: growth excluding currency moves and acquisitions.
- **Covered lives**: people enrolled in a benefits manager's plans.
- **Excepted benefit**: a benefit that can be offered outside a main health plan under US rules.
- **TrumpRx**: the US government's direct-to-consumer cash drug-pricing site.
- **Phase 3 interim readout**: an early look at data from the final stage of clinical trials.
- **IPO**: initial public offering. **EQT**: a Swedish private-equity firm. **52-week low**: the lowest share price of the past year.
