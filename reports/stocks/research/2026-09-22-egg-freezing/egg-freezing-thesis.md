# Single Women and Egg Freezing: How to Capitalise

*Research date 22 September 2026. Three research streams (demand and demographics; clinics, benefits managers and storage; drugs and lab supply chain) plus a devil's-advocate review. Raw reports with sources are in `raw/`. All figures are from web-search snippets because finance sites are blocked from this sandbox; verify against filings before acting. Not investment advice.*

---

## 1. The answer first

The thesis has the direction right and the timing wrong. Three facts matter most.

1. **The demographic premise is already true and has stopped moving.** Unmarried US women aged 15 and over have outnumbered married women since around 2010. Pew shows unpartnered US adults falling from 44% (2019) to 42% (2023), with married adults ticking up to 51%. Marriages rebounded in South Korea (+14.8% in 2024, +8.1% in 2025) and China (+10.8% in 2025). The real driver is later childbearing, not marital status.
2. **The market is small and growth just stalled.** US egg freezing grew from about 6,100 cycles in 2014 to about 38,000 in 2023, roughly 23% a year. SART's preliminary 2024 report shows 38,930 cycles, only about 2% growth. UK volumes were flat in 2024. At roughly $16,000 a cycle, US egg freezing is about $620M of revenue a year, of which about two thirds is elective.
3. **There is almost no listed pure play.** The companies that capture most of the money (US clinic chains, TMRW/Reprotech storage, Ferring, Maven, Carrot, Kindbody) are private. Among listed names, only Progyny (about 5 to 11% of revenue, not disclosed) and Kitazato (about 3 to 9%) plausibly have more than 5% of revenue from elective egg freezing. For Merck KGaA, Cooper, Vitrolife and Organon it is under 1%.

**The best listed exposure is Kitazato (368A.T)**, the Japanese maker of the Cryotop vitrification kit used to freeze eggs and embryos. It is a real chokepoint: about 65% of its sales come from vitrification, it has a reported 70% share in Europe, 80% in China and 85% in India, a 53% operating margin and a price of about 13 times earnings. Own it as a bet on all frozen-cycle IVF, not on egg freezing alone.

## 2. First principles: where the money in one egg-freezing journey goes

A US woman who freezes her eggs at 34 and pays herself spends roughly $33,500 over ten years in the base case.

| Stream | When | Expected spend | Who captures it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Egg retrieval (about 1.5 cycles at $16,000) | Year 0 | $24,000 (72%) | Clinic about $11,000 per cycle; drug makers $2,500 to $4,500; lab consumables $300 to $800 inside the clinic fee |
| Storage ($500 to $1,000 a year) | Years 1 to 9 | $5,300 (16%) | Clinic or offsite storage company; near-zero marginal cost |
| Thaw, fertilisation and transfer (only about 20% return) | Years 4 to 8 | $2,400 (7%) | Clinic, IVF-stage labs, genetic testing |
| Fresh IVF after a failed thaw | Years 5 to 10 | $1,800 (5%) | Clinic, drug makers |

Three implications follow. The clinic keeps most of the money. Storage is the best annuity in the chain, because specimens rarely move once frozen. And the later IVF cycle, often described as the big prize, is a small part of expected value, because only 6% (US, 5 to 7 years) to 28% (Australia and New Zealand, 10 years) of women ever come back for their eggs.

## 4. Why the bull case struggles (devil's-advocate findings)

- **Volume growth stopped in 2024** in the two best-measured markets, the US (+2%) and the UK (flat). Total US IVF cycles grew only 1.4% despite a record-low fertility rate of 1.57.
- **Prices are falling across the chain.** The TrumpRx deal cut the three-drug Merck regimen by up to 84%. Organon fertility fell 9% excluding currency. Low-cost clinics charge $6,000 to $7,600, and Cofertility freezes for free if half the eggs are donated. Offsite storage at $200 to $500 a year undercuts clinic storage at $500 to $1,000.
- **Policy helps medical infertility, not elective freezing.** The federal "excepted fertility benefit" rule and California's SB 729 target medical infertility. Employer-paid elective freezing is taxable income, about $4,400 of extra tax on a $20,000 benefit. Employer egg-freezing coverage rose only from 16% (2024) to 18% (2026).
- **The track record is poor.** Kindbody fell from a $1.8bn valuation to about $400M. Progyny trades at about $27. Monash IVF collapsed after clinical errors. Private-equity clinic multiples compressed from 11 to 15 times earnings (EBITDA) to 6 to 14 times.
- **Technology could cut drug use per cycle.** Gameto's Fertilo shortens hormone stimulation from about two weeks to 2 to 3 days; its US Phase 3 interim readout is due in late 2026. Growing eggs from stem cells is at least a decade away.

What the bull case gets right: age-related infertility will keep rising as first births move later (US mean age 27.5, South Korea about 33); egg freezing did grow about 23% a year from 2014 to 2023; storage is an excellent annuity; Kitazato is a genuine chokepoint; and subsidies in Tokyo, Seoul and Taiwan show governments leaning in.

## 5. How to capitalise, honestly

- **Own the chokepoint, not the narrative.** Kitazato is the only listed company with a dominant, high-margin position in freezing itself. It benefits from all frozen cycles (egg and embryo), which are the fastest-growing part of IVF.
- **Wait for Progyny to get cheap.** It is the only listed name with meaningful elective exposure, but falling use per member and client losses argue for buying near its 52-week low, not at $27.
- **Recognise that the best economics are private.** Clinic chains with owned storage (US Fertility, IVI-RMA) and storage consolidators (TMRW/Reprotech) capture the annuity. Public investors can only reach them through IPOs, and the first one (Indira IVF) is an exit by its owners.
- **Think about the wider thesis.** If the real driver is later childbearing, the broader beneficiaries are IVF volume and frozen-cycle labs, not elective egg freezing. That points back to Kitazato and, on a large drawdown, Vitrolife.
- **Avoid the drug makers for this thesis.** Egg freezing is under 1% of their revenue and their US prices are being cut.
