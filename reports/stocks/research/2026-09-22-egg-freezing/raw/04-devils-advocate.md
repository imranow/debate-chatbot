## (a) Strongest bear argument

The thesis takes a demographic shift that happened about 15 years ago and treats it as a new revenue stream. That stream is small and has just stopped growing:
- **Size.** US elective egg freezing brings in roughly **$0.4-0.6bn a year** [calc].
- **Growth.** It grew about **2% in 2024** (SART preliminary, 38,126 to 38,930 cycles), and UK volumes were flat in 2024.
- **Exposure.** Among the listed names, only Progyny (about 5-11%, undisclosed) and Kitazato (about 3-9%) plausibly get more than ~5% of revenue from it. For Merck KGaA, Cooper, Vitrolife, Organon and Richter it is **well under 1%**.
- **The pure plays are private, and their owners are selling.** US clinic chains, TMRW/Reprotech, Ferring, Maven, Carrot and Kindbody are all private. EQT's Indira IPO is 100% secondary shares, and US Fertility has been sold on.

What is left is squeezed on price: the TrumpRx deal cut the three-drug regimen 84% below list, Organon's fertility sales fell 9% ex-FX, and Progyny Rx grew only +1.2%. The wider IVF sector has also stopped growing even with record-low fertility rates:
- Progyny ART cycles +0.4%
- Vitrolife +1% organic
- Monash IVF revenue -0.9%
- Merck Fertility & Endocrinology about -5% and -1% organic in Q1 and Q2 2026

So demographics are not turning into revenue. And because only 6-28% of frozen eggs are ever used, the later IVF revenue is a small option that arrives years later.

## (c) Estimated share of revenue truly exposed to elective egg freezing

These are my assumptions [reasoning], not company disclosures:
- About two-thirds of egg-freezing cycles are elective (AJOG 2021: 16,436 ÷ 24,558 = 67%).
- Egg freezing is 9.0% of US ART cycles (38,930 ÷ 431,746).
- Weighted globally, elective egg freezing is about 2-4% of stimulated cycles. China effectively has zero; the UK is ~7% of cycles and Australia/NZ ~6%.
- An egg-freezing cycle uses about 30-60% of an IVF cycle's lab consumables, because there is no ICSI, embryo culture, time-lapse imaging or PGT.

## (e) What the bull case gets right

- **The long-run demand driver is real.** US mean age at first birth is 27.5 (2023). Korea's is about 33, and its total fertility rate was 0.80 (2025). Age-related infertility rises from these structurally.
- **Growth from 2014 to 2023 was real.** US egg freezing went from about 6,100 to about 38-39k cycles, roughly 23% a year. Employer egg-freezing coverage rose from 2% (2016) to 18% (2026) (IFEBP).
- **Storage is an attractive annuity.** It costs clinics close to nothing extra, and specimens rarely move because of the liability involved.
- **Kitazato is a real chokepoint at a fair price.** It has about a 53.5% operating margin, about 65% of sales from vitrification, and trades at about 13x P/E.
- **Policy is moving the right way, if narrowly.** The US excepted-benefit rule, California SB 729, and subsidies in Tokyo, Seoul and Taiwan all help.
