# Demand-side research: single women and egg freezing (as of 2026-09-22)

**Bottom line:**
- **Demographics.** On the Census "women 15+" measure, unmarried US women already outnumber married women. PRB put married women at 49.9%, and Wells Fargo had 52% unmarried or separated in 2021. But the trend has stalled. Pew shows unpartnered adults fell from 44% in 2019 to 42% in 2023, and married adults ticked up to 51%. Marriages also rebounded in 2024-25 in Korea (+14.8%, then +8.1%) and China (+10.8% in 2025).
- **Volumes.** US egg freezing grew at about 23% a year from 2014 to 2023, reaching 39,269 cycles in 2023. The UK flattened in 2024, the first year without growth since 2020.
- **Unit economics.** Frozen eggs are rarely used, so a customer's value comes mostly from the retrieval and the storage fees. The later IVF cycle is a large bill when it happens, but only a minority of women ever come back for it.
- **Payers and policy.** The tailwinds are real but narrow. Most mandates and the federal "excepted benefit" proposal target *medical* infertility, not elective freezing.

Research limits: WebFetch was blocked for every domain I tried (HFEA, SART, medRxiv, Cofertility, Berry). Every number below comes from search snippets and needs checking against the primary source before publication.

- 6,460 egg freezing cycles in 2021.

## (c) Unit economics per customer over 10 years

**Inputs:**
- **Price per cycle:** $12-20k all-in nationally; $15-20k in big cities; average about $16k ($11k clinic + $5k drugs) per FertilityIQ. New York runs $20-25k (CNBC, Aug 2026).
- **Low-cost end:** Kindbody $6k before drugs; CNY Fertility $7,594 all-in; Cofertility is free if the woman donates half her eggs.
- **Storage:** $500-1,000 a year (FertilityIQ).
- **Cycles per woman:** FertilityIQ says the average patient does 2 and over 20% do 3. The ANZ data says most women do a single procedure. I used **1.5** as the base case.
- **Lifetime cost split (FertilityIQ):** about 70% treatment, 10% drugs, 20% storage.
- **Using the eggs later:** thaw $1-2.5k, ICSI $1.5-3k, transfer $3-5.5k, monitoring $0.3-0.8k, so $5.8-11.8k per attempt. One transfer is about $7.5k; with genetic testing (PGT) and 3 transfers it approaches $20k. A fresh IVF cycle is about $23k (Forbes, 2026).

**How many women come back:**

| Study | Share who returned | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| US SART-CORS, 2014-16 freezers (AJOG 2025) | 5.7% (about 8% for women who froze at 38-42) | 5-7 years |
| Extend Fertility | about 9% (271 of ~3,000) | 0-7 years |
| Australia/NZ, 2009-13 freezers | 27.9%, average 4.9 years after freezing | 10+ years |
| Tokyo (East Asia Forum) | ~15% | Not stated |

**What happens when they come back:**
- 21.5% of US returners had no usable embryo (AJOG).
- Share of women with at least one live birth: 39% (NYU 2022); 51% if they froze before 38; 70% if under 38 and more than 20 mature eggs thawed; about one-third in Australia/NZ.
- UK cumulative live birth rate 34%, 45% if eggs were frozen before 36 (2024 study).

**Worked example (base case):** US woman freezes at 34, self-pay, $16k per cycle, 1.5 cycles.

| Stream | Timing | Calculation | Expected $ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retrieval | Year 0 | 1.5 × $16k (first-year storage included) | 24,000 |
| Storage | Years 1-9 | $750 a year × 95% of women still storing each year (sum ≈ 7.0) | 5,270 |
| Thaw, ICSI and transfer | Years 4-8 | 20% return × $12k | 2,400 |
| Fresh IVF after a failed thaw | Years 5-10 | 20% × 40% fail × $23k | 1,840 |
| **Total** | | | **≈ $33.5k** |

The split is about 72% retrieval, 16% storage and 12% later treatment. The later IVF cycle is the biggest single bill *when it happens*, but at US return rates of 6-28% it is a small part of expected value.

**Scenarios:**
- **Bull** (employer-paid, 2 cycles, 30% return, $15k thaw path, 50% of returners go on to fresh IVF): about $45k.
- **Bear** (a single discounted cycle of about $8k, $500 a year storage, 10% return): about $13.6k.

**Rough US storage estimate (my own):** about 170k cycles between 2014 and 2023 (2015-17 interpolated), divided by 1.5 cycles per woman, gives about 115k women. If 70% are still storing, that is about 80k women × $750, or roughly $60m a year of recurring storage revenue.

## (d) Payer and policy tailwinds

**US employers:**
- **Mercer:** IVF coverage at large employers (500+ staff) went from 45% in 2023 to 47% in 2024. Among the largest (20,000+) it went from 62% to 70%. Elective egg freezing is covered by 21% of large employers. A snippet also says 19% of the largest employers cover it, which looks garbled (flagged in f). Mercer says most employers planned to keep benefits unchanged in 2025.
- **IFEBP:** egg freezing coverage rose from 2% (2016) to 6% (2018), 10% (2020), 14% (2022) and 16% (2024). Any fertility benefit rose from 30% (2020) to 40% (2022) and 42% (2024).
- **SHRM 2025:** 24% of employers cover IVF.

**US federal:**
- **Executive Order 14216 (18 Feb 2025):** asked for IVF access and cost recommendations within 90 days. It contained no mandate.
- **16 Oct 2025:** EMD Serono agreed to sell Gonal-f, Ovidrel and Cetrotide at a discount through TrumpRx. The company claims up to 84% savings if a patient uses all three. The FDA is to fast-track Pergoveris. The government issued guidance encouraging standalone fertility benefits, with no mandate or subsidy.
- **Proposed rule, "Excepted Fertility Benefits" (issued 10 May, published 13 May 2026):**
  - Lets employers offer fertility cover as a standalone benefit, like dental or vision.
  - Would apply to plan years starting on or after 1 Jan 2027; comments closed 13 Jul 2026.
  - Covers diagnosis and treatment of *medical infertility*, so elective freezing is probably excluded (the rule text needs checking).
- **Tax headwind:** employer-paid elective fertility benefits are taxable income. On a $20k benefit, a woman earning $80k owes about $4.4k more tax (CNBC, Aug 2026).

**US states:**
- **California SB 729:** applies to fully insured large-group plans (101+ employees) for contracts issued or renewed from 1 Jan 2026. It covers up to 3 egg retrievals and unlimited transfers, and uses a definition of infertility that includes single and LGBTQ+ people. It covers *medically necessary* freezing only, not elective. It excludes self-funded plans, and CalPERS starts 1 Jul 2027.
- **New York S4497/A7339:** would mandate cover for elective freezing; still pending.
- About 16 states have fertility mandates (market-report claim; flagged in f).

**UK:** The NHS does not fund elective egg freezing. NHS-funded IVF fell from 35% of cycles in 2019 to 28% in 2024 (25% in England), which reinforces self-pay.

**Asia:**

| Place | Scheme |
|---|---|
| Tokyo | Up to ¥300k (about $2k) for women 18-39, no cap on numbers, expanded April 2024 |
| Seoul | 50% of costs up to ₩2m for women 20-49; 650 recipients; AMH (egg-reserve test) threshold for women in their 20s relaxed |
| Taiwan | New Taipei NT$30k from July 2025; about 12 cities or counties have schemes; national medical-freezing subsidy up to NT$70k (cancer patients), starting early 2026 |
| Singapore | Legalised July 2023; age limit 37 |
| China | Banned for single women. A Beijing court upheld the ban in October 2025 (Xu Zaozao case). A Shanghai CPPCC member proposed a Pudong pilot in 2025. No change found at the 2026 Two Sessions |

## (e) Contrarian risks

1. **Few women use their eggs.** US return rates are 5.7% at 5-7 years; Extend is about 9%; Australia/NZ about 28% at 10 years. This caps the later IVF revenue and brings regulatory and reputational scrutiny ("most will never use them," 19th News and UCLA, Sep-Oct 2025). In the UK, eggs going into storage far outpace eggs being used or disposed of.
2. **Growth may be peaking.** UK volumes were flat in 2024. US unpartnered adults fell from 44% to 42% (Pew called singledom "peaked"). Marriages rebounded in Korea (+14.8%, +8.1%) and China (+10.8%). More marriages could shift demand from egg freezing to embryo freezing or regular IVF.
3. **Prices are falling.** Kindbody charges $6k, CNY Fertility $7.6k, Cofertility is free with egg-sharing, and TrumpRx cuts drug costs (about $5k of a $16k cycle). Revenue per cycle could drop 30-50% at the low end.
4. **Marketing to younger women.** Seoul relaxed its criteria for women in their 20s; Singapore has 4% under 30. Younger freezers come back least, so they pay storage fees but rarely buy the later IVF cycle. Ethics and advertising-regulation risk.
5. **Policy.** China's ban is intact, so upside there is optional only. The federal excepted-benefit rule and SB 729 are focused on infertility; elective freezing stays taxable; employers are holding benefits flat. Korea's 2024-26 doctors' strike disrupted services.
6. **Clinical outcomes.** About one-third of women get a live birth per ANZ data, 39% per NYU, and 21.5% of US returners have no usable embryo. Success depends heavily on age and number of eggs, so there is litigation risk if marketing overstates outcomes. Storage-tank failures are a known risk: from memory, 2018 incidents at Pacific Fertility and UH Cleveland (not verified this session).
7. **Stated demand vs actual.** Tokyo had 7,000 registrants but only about 1,800 applied. Singapore had only about 800 women in 2.5 years despite a TFR of 0.87. Subsidies bring interest faster than procedures.
