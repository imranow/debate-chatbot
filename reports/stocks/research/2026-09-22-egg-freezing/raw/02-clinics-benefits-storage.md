**Where the margin sits (my analysis, not sourced):**
- **Lab consumables have the highest percentage margins.** Vitrolife runs a 34.4% EBITDA margin on about 60% gross margin, but the dollars per cycle are small.
- **Drugs are high-margin but under US price pressure.** EMD Serono agreed an 84% TrumpRx discount on its three-drug regimen.
- **Clinics capture the most absolute dollars.** They take about $11k of a roughly $16k cycle, at about 20–25% EBITDA margins (Monash 19.8%, Jinxin 24.5%).
- **Clinics also own the best annuity: storage.** They charge $500–1,000 a year, the incremental cost is close to zero, and many eggs are never used.
- **FBMs take roughly a 25% gross spread on pass-through billings, but only for covered members.** Progyny's gross margin is 25.5% and its adjusted EBITDA margin is 17.7%.
- **This matters for the thesis.** Egg freezing by single women is still mostly self-pay, so FBMs capture a minority of that spend. Clinics and storage capture most of it.
- **Best leverage to the thesis:** clinic platforms with owned storage (US Fertility, IVI-RMA, Inception), consolidated offsite storage (TMRW/Reprotech), and Progyny only to the extent that employers add egg-freezing coverage.

## (b) Detail with sourced figures

### 1. Fertility benefits managers

**Progyny (PGNY)**

*Q2 2026 (reported about Aug 6–7, 2026):*
- Revenue $350.5M, up 5.3% year on year (from $332.9M). Excluding the departed large client, growth was 11.0%.
- Gross margin 25.5%, up 180bp from 23.7%.
- Adjusted EBITDA $62.1M, up 7.2%, a 17.7% margin (17.4% a year earlier).
- 604 clients (542 a year earlier) and 7.185M covered lives (6.743M).
- ART cycles 16,998 versus 16,938, up only 0.4% **[calc]** while covered lives grew about 6.6%. **Utilization per member is falling.**

*Guidance:*
- **FY2026 revenue $1.360–1.385B** (+5.5% to +7.5%; +9.7% to +11.7% excluding $48.5M of H1 2025 transition-client revenue).
- The Q1 print in May had guided $1.365–1.405B, with adjusted EBITDA $232–244M and net income $103.7–112.3M.
- The February guide had adjusted EBITDA at $224–239M.
- **Conflict:** one headline says Q2 "raised" 2026 guidance. The snippets instead show the revenue range was narrowed and its top end cut. I could not see the August EBITDA guide.
- Q3 2026 revenue guide: $335–345M.
- The stock fell sharply after hours on the Q2 print. Management cited a "more pronounced" mid-summer seasonal slowdown. Simply Wall St reported the shares down 14.3%.

*FY2025:*
- Fertility benefit services revenue $830.9M (+14%). Pharmacy (Progyny Rx) $457.7M (+4.6%).
- Total ≈ $1,288.6M **[calc]**, with pharmacy about 35.5% of revenue **[calc]**.
- Operating cash flow a record $210.2M. About 6.5M shares repurchased. Q2 2026 commentary says the buyback authorization was completed.

*Client losses:*
- Amazon (about 12–13% of revenue, roughly $130M of 2023 revenue and about 670k lives) left for **Maven** effective 1 January 2025.
- A transition-of-care agreement added $44–46M of revenue in 2025. It ended mid-2025, and the client fully rolled off by Q1 2026.

*2027 selling season:* management says early new-client commitments are ahead of last year, "retention risks have largely been addressed," and it targets at least 1M new covered lives.

*Egg freezing:*
- The share of cycles that are egg freezing is **not disclosed**. ART cycles combine IVF, embryo banking and egg freezing.
- Progyny marketing says more than 50% of its egg freezers have some coverage.

*Valuation (as of about Sep 18, 2026):*
- Price $26.86, market cap about $2.10B.
- P/E 29.8x trailing and 18.8x forward. 52-week range $16.10–33.07.
- Consensus target about $34.42 (range $30–40, 10 Buys).
- Market cap to FY26 adjusted EBITDA midpoint of about $238M is roughly 8.8x **[calc]**. Enterprise value is lower after net cash, but the cash balance was not captured (**missing**).

*M&A:* I found no credible takeover rumour. Commentary only calls it a "potential target" for insurers or PBMs. Management talks about tuck-ins and buybacks.

*Policy:* on Oct 16, 2025, DOL, HHS and Treasury issued guidance, followed by a proposed rule, that lets employers offer fertility as a standalone "excepted benefit," similar to dental or vision. That is a structural tailwind for FBMs. HR Brew doubts it will move adoption quickly.

*Biggest risk:* losing jumbo clients to bundled women's-health platforms (Maven), and falling utilization per member.

- Valued at $1.7B at its $125M Series F (Oct 2024, led by StepStone). It has raised more than $425M in total.
- More than 2,000 employer and health-plan relationships across 175 countries.
- Won Amazon's roughly 1.5M employees across 50 countries.
- Launched a direct-to-consumer platform in March 2026.
- IPO rumoured for 2026; no filing found.
- Moat: breadth across maternity, menopause and fertility, which wins single-vendor RFPs.

**Carrot Fertility (private)**

- Revenue about $88M per Owler (low reliability).

**Kindbody (private; distressed)**
- Valuation has fallen from $1.8B to a targeted $600M pre-money raise, and reportedly possibly as low as about $400M.

- Considering a sale (Bloomberg Law; date unclear). CEO turnover.

### 2. Clinic networks

**Egg-freezing volume context (SART):** 24,560 cycles in 2021, 29,803 in 2022, and about 40,000 in 2023 (+39%). Total US ART cycles in 2023 were 415,953.

**Consumer economics:**
- Cycle price $12–20k: about $11k procedure plus about $5k drugs. NYC and LA run $16–24k.
- About 2 cycles on average, and more than 20% of patients need 3.
- Clinic storage $500–1,000 a year ($1,500 at premium clinics). Offsite storage $200–500. The first year is often bundled.

**US Fertility (Amulet + L Catterton + Shady Grove physicians)**
- 121 clinics and labs in 20 states, more than 200 physicians. Merged with Ovation (lab network) and partnered with RMA (2023).
- L Catterton bought 42.5% from Amulet; physicians and management keep about 15%. Closed Dec 30, 2025.
- Financing: $825M first-lien term loan B due 2032 (SOFR + 350bp) plus $1.71B of new cash and rollover equity. That implies total capitalization of roughly $2.5B+ **[calc; not a confirmed EV]**.
- About $1B of revenue expected in 2026. S&P affirmed its B- rating (Dec 3, 2025).
- EBITDA is **not disclosed**. At an assumed 18–22% margin, the implied multiple would be about 11–14x **[calc/assumption]**.

**IVI-RMA (KKR)**
- KKR bought it in April 2022 for about $3B (another source says $3.8B).
- Add-ons: Eugin/Boston IVF about $535M; ART Fertility (Middle East/India) $400–450M.
- 2024 refinancing: €565M plus $500M of term loans and a €234M revolver.
- LeadIQ puts revenue at $250–500M, which looks too low for the platform's scale (**unreliable**).

**Other US platforms**
- **Inception/Prelude:** includes Aspire (11 Texas sites plus Atlanta). Exit preparation reported. Current owner and financials **not confirmed**.
- **CCRM:** folded into Unified Women's Healthcare for $775M (Sep 2023). Unified is backed by Altas, Ares PE and Oak HC/FT.
- **Pinnacle:** owned by Webster Equity. Bankers have discussed an exit for about 12 months, with no named buyer as of mid-2026.
- **Spring Fertility:** private. Bought Genesis Fertility Centre (Vancouver) in 2020. New COO and CFO in 2026 for a "national expansion."
- **Extend Fertility:** raised about $15M. Expanded to 14k sq ft at 200 W 57th St (Oct 2025). In-network with Aetna, Cigna and GHI.
- **Cofertility:** $7.25M Series A (Apr 2025). Freeze-for-free if you donate half the eggs.

**PE multiples**
- US fertility clinics in 2026 trade at about 6–14x EBITDA. By size: single-MD practices 5–7x, regional groups 7–10x, mid-size platforms 9–12x, premium scale platforms 10–14x+ (CT Acquisitions).
- Historically 11–15x, now at the low end; many small deals at 7–9x (Fertility Bridge / ION).
- 2026's theme is PE exits. The US Fertility sale is the bellwether.

**Monash IVF (MVF.AX)**
- FY26 (June year-end): revenue A$269.5M (-0.9%). Underlying EBITDA A$53.4M, margin 19.8% (down 460bp). Underlying NPAT A$16.1M, which missed guidance of A$17–18M.
- 11,423 stimulated cycles. Australian market share 20.2%.
- Two embryo mix-ups (human error and IT) triggered the share collapse.
- The board rejected a Genesis Capital + Washington H. Soul Pattinson bid: A$0.80 in Nov 2025, then A$0.90 (about A$350M) in April 2026. The consortium holds 19.6%.
- Share price about A$0.755–0.79, market cap about A$308M (Sep 2026).
- Market cap to EBITDA about 5.8x **[calc; net debt missing]**.
- Biggest risk: clinical-governance and brand damage, and share loss.

**Virtus Health:** taken private by BGH Capital in Aug 2022. Owns TFP (UK), plus operations in Ireland, Denmark and Singapore. No 2026 financials.

**CARE Fertility (UK):** PitchBook lists Nordic Capital as investor. No 2025–26 sale data found (**gap**).

**Jinxin Fertility (1951.HK)**
- H1 2026 revenue RMB1.37B (+6.6%). Net profit RMB110.3M, against a RMB1.04B loss a year earlier (likely an impairment).
- Gross margin 37.4% (from 30.4%). Adjusted EBITDA RMB336.5M (+49.8%), margin 24.5%.
- US unit (HRC Fertility) is gaining traction.
- Market cap about HK$6.13B at HK$2.27 (early Sep 2026). Annualized market cap to EBITDA about 8x **[calc; debt ignored]**.
- **The thesis does not apply in mainland China:** the National Health Commission bans unmarried women from ART, including egg freezing. 27 provinces reimburse ART for married couples.
- CITIC-Xiangya (Changsha) holds about 800,000 frozen embryos; it is not listed.

**Indira IVF (India)**
- SEBI approved the IPO on Dec 30, 2025, after a confidential refiling. The issue is about ₹3,500 crore (about $408M) and entirely secondary: EQT sells about ₹29bn, and three Murdia family members sell about ₹2bn each.
- **Not listed as of the latest results found.**
- Nova IVF: no IPO news found.
- Gaudium IVF did a small-cap Indian IPO in 2026 (details unverified).

### 3. Cryostorage and logistics

**TMRW Life Sciences + Reprotech (private)**
- Combination announced April 2026. The combined company holds more than 1M specimens, partners with about 75% of US fertility centers, supports more than 50% of US patient cycles, and runs 8 offsite facilities.
- TMRW has the only FDA-cleared automated storage platform for eggs and embryos, with RFID chain of custody. One unit replaces 20–25 dewars and saves 70% of space.
- TMRW raised more than $170M since 2018, including a $28M+ Series D (Jul 2024, led by 5AM). It bought Cryogatt's patents (2025) and launched the TMRW Vault (Mar 2025).
- Moat: liability-driven switching costs. Once specimens are stored, they rarely move.

**Cryoport (CYRX)**
- Q2 2026 revenue $49M. First positive adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations. FY26 guide $192–196M.
- Life Sciences Services +15%, BioStorage +25%.
- Reproductive medicine revenue is **not broken out** and is immaterial to the thesis.

**CryoFuture, FertilitySafe:** no data found (**gap**).

**Liability precedent**
- Pacific Fertility Center (San Francisco) tank failure on Mar 4, 2018 affected about 3,500 eggs and embryos.
- In June 2021 a jury awarded $14.975M to five plaintiffs against the tank maker, Chart Industries, finding a manufacturing defect and failure to recall.
- The appeal was settled out of court (Mar 2023).
- Related risk: CooperSurgical's recalled culture media. More than 140 lawsuits and more than 1,500 claimants; settlements have been largely covered by insurance.

**Storage economics (my analysis)**
- At about $750 a year, one patient is worth roughly $5–10k of near-100%-incremental-margin revenue over 7–15 years in-clinic **[calc/assumption]**.
- This is the most "annuity-like" slice of the egg-freezing value chain.
- Offsite consolidators undercut clinics at $200–500 a year.
