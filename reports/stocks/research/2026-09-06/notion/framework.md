## How to read this page
- Each name is a **toggle**: the coloured box at the top gives the skeptic's verdict (green compelling, blue interesting, yellow weak, red avoid), how far the stock has already run, risk and suggested sizing. Open the toggle for the full dossier: numbers, guidance, valuation, catalysts, bull and bear case, what confirms and what kills the thesis, the skeptic's corrections, and sources.
- "How far it has run" is the single most important field. *Early* means the thesis is not yet in the price. *Mid* means partly. *Late* means it is mostly priced in and you would be buying the crowd's idea.
- Names that also went through the independent verification sweep show the three refuters' verdicts (priced-in, durability, evidence). Two of three refutations means the sweep rejected it.

**The rule, from first principles.** A share price is earnings times a multiple. A boom needs both to move at once. A bottleneck does it by giving a supplier pricing power: revenue rises with price while costs do not, so earnings rise by multiples, and the market then pays more per dollar of those earnings because they look durable. A binary does it by changing the earnings picture in a single step. Neither works if the market has already priced it in, which is why every dossier below is scored on how far the stock has already run.
**Why the primary memory trade is now late.** By early September 2026 mainstream outlets were asking whether the memory supercycle peak was near, and Micron's own 2027 capacity was reported fully booked. When the crowd is debating the peak, the next leg usually comes from adjacent suppliers that have not re-rated: controllers, specialty memory, packaging, test, substrates, lasers, transformers.
## 2. The five signatures we search for, in order of reliability
1. **"Sold out", "fully allocated", "lead times extended", "capacity booked through 2027"** in earnings calls and trade press, especially from small and mid caps. Price follows allocation with a one to two quarter lag.
2. **Second-tier suppliers raising guidance after the leader** (Silicon Motion after SanDisk; Amkor and ASE after TSMC; Hammond after GE Vernova). The leader's re-rating is the map; the second tier is the territory that has not been walked yet.
3. **Announced contract or list price increases** (memory, NOR, HDDs, storage arrays, transformers, gensets). A price increase is the earliest hard evidence of pricing power.
4. **Dated binary events on washed-out charts** with high short interest: readouts, FDA decisions, first launches, contract awards.
5. **Cluster insider buying** (three or more insiders) in the same sectors as 1 to 3.
## 3. Rules for using this list
- **Size by tier and by "how far run".** Early-stage bottleneck names with a compelling skeptic verdict can be core positions in a speculative sleeve (2 to 3% each). Mid-stage names 1 to 2%. Late-stage names are watchlist only: buy the pullback, never the breakout. Binary events are lottery tickets at 0.5 to 1%, sized so a total loss is a flesh wound.
- **The whole sleeve stays at 10 to 15% of the portfolio.** The core stays in index funds and the compounders on the "Stocks to Pick" page. Never fund a speculation by selling a compounder.
- **Enter on confirmation, not on story.** Each dossier names the datapoint that confirms the thesis. Wait for it, or for the price screener (the debate-chatbot repo's stock screener) to show the name entering the breakout watchlist with volume.
- **Exit on the kill condition, not on the price.** Each dossier names what would refute the thesis. If it happens, sell regardless of the chart.
- **Re-verify dates.** Catalyst dates slip. Check the company's investor-relations page the week before.
