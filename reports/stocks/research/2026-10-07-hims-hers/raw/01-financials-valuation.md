### 3.2 Customer acquisition cost (CAC)
Hims does not disclose CAC per customer or gross adds. Upper-bound estimate:
- Q2 2026 marketing $262.2M ÷ ~300k net adds = **~$874 per net add** (estimate). Net adds understate gross adds (churned subscribers must also be replaced), so true CAC per gross add is lower. Eucalyptus subscribers may also be inside the 300k, which would flatter this.
- Gross-profit payback (estimate): $92 × 64% = $58.9 gross profit per subscriber-month. $874 / $58.9 = **~15 months** on the net-add basis. On a per-gross-add basis payback is shorter. Treat as an upper bound.

### 3.5 Gross margin by product type — first principles (all estimates)

The reason compounded and branded margins differ is **who owns the molecule's price**.

- **Compounded semaglutide (pre-March 2026).** Hims's own pharmacies bought generic semaglutide active ingredient and filled vials. Ingredient, vial, compounding labor and shipping cost perhaps $30–50 a month. Hims sold the whole package (doctor visit + drug) at about $199–249 a month. Gross margin ≈ (199 − 40) / 199 = **~80%**. Hims captured almost all the value because there was no brand owner in the chain.
- **Branded Wegovy/Ozempic (from March 2026).** Novo sets the price. Under the deal, Hims sells at "the same price as other telehealth platforms" [S20]. If Hims books the drug at gross (it dispenses through its own pharmacy, so it likely acts as principal), the drug cost is a pass-through. Illustration: injectable at a ~$349 cash price plus a ~$149 monthly membership = ~$498 revenue. Cost ≈ $349 drug + ~$15 fulfillment and clinician = $364. Gross margin ≈ (498 − 364) / 498 = **~27%**. For the oral Wegovy pill at ~$149 plus $149 membership: (298 − 164) / 298 = **~45%**. Retail prices come from consumer guides (Healthline, telehealthally) and conflict ($149–$299 for the pill) (unverified). Hims's purchase price from Novo is not disclosed.
- **Legacy categories (sexual health, hair, dermatology).** Generic sildenafil, tadalafil, finasteride and minoxidil cost cents to a few dollars a month. Gross margin is likely **85–90%** (estimate), which is how Hims ran at 76–82% gross margin before weight loss scaled.
- **International (Eucalyptus, Zava).** Management says it carries lower gross margin. Eucalyptus sells branded GLP-1s in Australia/UK, so the same pass-through logic applies.

Net effect: branded weight loss lifts revenue per subscriber and gross profit dollars per subscriber, but cuts gross margin %. Management said gross margin "will remain below the levels we have historically achieved" [S10]. The model below uses EBITDA margin, not gross margin, so this shows up as a 2030 margin assumption well below the old 20% target in the base case.

## 6. First-principles 2030 valuation

Model file: `/home/user/debate-chatbot/reports/stocks/research/2026-10-07-hims-hers/raw/01-valuation_model.py`

**Chain.** 2030 subscribers × annual revenue per subscriber = revenue. × adj. EBITDA margin = EBITDA. × exit EV/EBITDA = enterprise value at end-2030. Add 2030 cash (today's cash + cumulative FCF − Eucalyptus cash payments), less convertible debt (unless the notes are in the money, then they convert to shares). ÷ diluted shares = value per share. Implied annual return = (value / $29.40)^(1/4.23) − 1, where 4.23 years = 7 Oct 2026 to 31 Dec 2030. No dividends.

**Fixed inputs (reported unless noted).** Price $29.40 (snippet); basic shares 233.3M; cash $841M; 2030 notes $1,000M at $70.67; 2032 notes $402.5M at $29.53; Eucalyptus deferred $710M, 60% in stock at today's price, 40% cash (estimate of election); today 2.891M subs × $1,104 a year ($92 × 12) = $3.19B run-rate.

**Scenario assumptions (all estimates).**

| | Bear | Base | Bull |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2030 subscribers | 3.5M (+21% total vs 2.89M; ~4.6%/yr) | 4.5M (~11%/yr) | 5.8M (~18%/yr) |
| Annual revenue per subscriber | $1,000 (branded price cuts, compounded gone, mix to cheaper generics) | $1,150 (≈ today's $1,104 + modest mix) | $1,250 (more multi-category, labs, hormones) |
| Adj. EBITDA margin | 8% (≈ Q2 2026) | 14% (≈ FY2025) | 20% (management target) |
| Exit EV / EBITDA | 10x | 14x | 18x |
| Cumulative FCF H2-26 to 2030 | $0 | $1.2B | $2.5B |
| Net annual share growth (SBC after buybacks) | 3.0% | 2.0% | 1.5% |
| Eucalyptus earn-out paid | $0 | $100M | $200M |

Why these: bear assumes branded GLP-1s become a low-margin pass-through and competition (Novo/Lilly direct-to-consumer, Amazon, Costco) caps growth. Base assumes the business grows near its pre-GLP-1 pace and margins return to 2025 levels. Bull is roughly management's 2030 plan ($7.25B revenue vs ≥$6.5B target).

Return = (38.26 / 29.40)^(1/4.23) − 1 = 6.4% a year.

**What the model says.**
1. Today's price is roughly a "base case minus a little" bet. A 0% return needs about **3.4M subscribers at a 14% margin and 14x**, or **4.5M subscribers at a 10.5% margin**. A 10% annual return needs about **5.2M subscribers** (14% margin) or a **16% margin** (4.5M subs).
2. Hitting management's 2030 plan ($6.5B, 20%) gives **~21% a year at 14x**, and still 12.5% at a 10x multiple. The stock prices in substantial doubt about the plan.
3. The margin is the swing factor. Each 3 points of 2030 EBITDA margin moves the base-case return by ~4–5 points a year. Branded GLP-1 pass-through revenue is the main reason the margin could stay near 8–11%.
4. The bear case is severe (−29% a year, ~$7/share) because a 2.8B EV leaves little after $1.4B of converts and Eucalyptus payments. In the bear case the May 2030 notes ($1B) would need refinancing.
5. Dilution is material: 233M → ~279–295M shares by 2030 across scenarios (SBC, Eucalyptus stock, 2032 conversion).

Model limits: one-period exit, no discounting of interim cash flows, Eucalyptus stock priced at today's price, SBC treated only as dilution (the EBITDA multiple is applied to adjusted EBITDA, which excludes SBC). Revenue per subscriber already mixes in pass-through drug revenue, so subs × $/sub is not a clean measure of value per customer.
