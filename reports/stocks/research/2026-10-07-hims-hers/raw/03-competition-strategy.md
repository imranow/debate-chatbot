Stream 03 of 3 (competition, moat, strategy). Prepared 7 October 2026.
Other streams cover financials/valuation and GLP-1 regulation/legal. This file touches those only where they shape competition.

## How to read this file

- Every fact has a date and a source number in square brackets, for example [S1]. The source list is at the end.
- "(unverified)" means only one source, or a low-quality source, supports the claim.
- "(our view)" marks our own judgement, not a reported fact.
- Method note: web search worked. Direct page fetches were blocked by the sandbox for sec.gov, investors.hims.com, nasdaq.com, fool.com, fiercehealthcare.com and tradingview.com. So most facts come from search-engine summaries of those pages, not from reading the full filings. Treat exact figures as needing a check against the 10-Q before publication.

Glossary (first use):
- **DTC**: direct-to-consumer. The company sells straight to patients online, not through a doctor's office or insurer.
- **GLP-1**: a class of weight-loss and diabetes drugs (semaglutide = Wegovy/Ozempic; tirzepatide = Zepbound/Mounjaro).
- **Compounding**: a pharmacy mixing its own version of a drug. Legal in narrow cases (for example shortages or a patient-specific need). Not FDA-approved.
- **Branded**: the FDA-approved product made by the patent holder (Novo Nordisk, Eli Lilly).
- **Cash-pay / self-pay**: the patient pays without insurance.
- **CAC**: customer acquisition cost, the marketing spend needed to win one paying customer.
- **Moat**: a lasting advantage that stops rivals from taking a firm's customers or profits.

## 1. Moat from first principles

A DTC telehealth brand sells three things: a fast online doctor visit, a drug, and convenience (home delivery, refills, chat). None of these is scarce on its own. Durability has to come from something a rival cannot copy cheaply. We test seven candidate sources.

### 1.1 Brand
- Evidence for: Hims ran Super Bowl ads in 2025 and again in February 2026. The 2026 ad ("The Era of Health as a Luxury is Over") was announced 29 January 2026 [S10][S11]. One report says web traffic rose 650% within hours of the 2026 ad, and the Hers and Hims apps reached No. 2 and No. 4 in Apple's Health & Wellness chart in the days after (unverified; reported via search summary of Forbes/other coverage) [S11][S23].
- Evidence against: Ro answered with its own first Super Bowl ad, starring Serena Williams, in February 2026 [S5][S6]. Amazon, Costco and the drug makers themselves now market GLP-1 access [S16][S17][S9]. In July 2026 the FTC and two states sued Hims over data sharing and hard-to-cancel subscriptions [S19]. In August 2026 Visa put Hims in a monitoring programme for excess card disputes [S20]. These hurt trust, which is what a health brand sells.
- Copyable? Awareness can be bought by anyone with a big budget. Trust takes years and can be lost fast (our view).

### 1.2 Customer acquisition cost advantage
- Evidence for: one third-party call review says marketing fell to 34% of revenue in Q2 2026 from 39% a year earlier (unverified) [S24]. A larger subscriber base and a known brand should lower CAC over time (our view).
- Evidence against: 34% of revenue is still very high for a company that wants to be a durable platform (our view). Morgan Stanley app data (date unclear; reported via TipRanks/The Fly) showed the first year-on-year fall in monthly app downloads, to about 185,000, and web traffic growth of only 5% in a June (unverified; year not confirmed) [S25]. LifeMD said in August 2026 that customer acquisition costs were higher "for much of the quarter" [S12]. That suggests paid channels for GLP-1 patients are crowded and pricey (our view).
- Copyable? Not a moat today. Scale gives some media-buying leverage. But every rival can bid on the same ads.

### 1.3 Vertical integration (pharmacies, compounding, labs, manufacturing)
- Hims bought Trybe Labs (Sigmund NJ LLC), an at-home blood-test lab in New Jersey, in February 2025, for about $5.1 million in cash per the 10-K [S13][S14].
- Hims bought a peptide manufacturing facility in California in February 2025; the 10-K links it to C S Bio [S15][S14]. Q2 2026 coverage says management was validating peptide ingredients at the Menlo Park facility while waiting for FDA decisions on compounding certain peptides [S13][S3].
- Hims runs affiliated pharmacies for fulfilment (our understanding from prior filings; not re-checked in this run).
- What this buys: lower cost per order, control of supply, and the ability to make personalised doses (our view).
- What it does not buy: the main profit pool, GLP-1s, has moved to branded drugs. Novo and Lilly make those. Hims cannot make them, only dispense them. Gross margin fell from 76% in Q2 2025 to 64% in Q2 2026, which management linked to the mix shift to branded weight loss and international revenue [S1][S2]. So in-house manufacturing helps less in the branded era (our view).
- LifeMD also has an in-house pharmacy and reported about 89% gross margin in Q2 2026 [S12]. Vertical integration is common, not unique.
- Copyable? Partly. Buying a pharmacy or lab is cheap ($5.1m for Trybe). A licensed peptide plant with FDA-compliant processes is harder, but only valuable if FDA lets peptides be compounded (our view).

### 1.4 Personalisation and data
- Hims said on the Q4 2025 call that 65% of subscribers, about 1.6 million people, used personalised treatments [S3]. In Q2 2025 it was nearly 1.5 million, up 89% year on year [S3].
- MedMatch is a provider-facing AI tool launched in beta in November 2023, first for anxiety and depression, to help clinicians choose formulation and dose [S26][S27].
- Weakness: "personalised" mostly meant compounded combinations and doses. The March 2026 Novo deal restricts compounded GLP-1 marketing [S7][S8]. So the main personalised product has been reduced (our view).
- The July 2026 FTC complaint alleges Hims shared sensitive health data with advertisers [S19]. Data is an asset only if customers trust how it is used.
- Copyable? Mostly. Any rival with doctors can offer a compounded combo where allowed. The data set is large, but there is no public proof it gives better outcomes (our view).

### 1.5 Subscription lock-in
- Hims had about 2.891 million subscribers at end-Q2 2026, up 19% from about 2.439 million [S1][S2]. Q2 includes the Eucalyptus acquisition, which closed in June 2026 [S1][S4]. We could not confirm how many acquired subscribers are in the count (gap).
- Lock-in is weak by design. Patients can switch to Ro, Amazon, LillyDirect or NovoCare with a new online visit. The drug is the same branded product everywhere (our view).
- The FTC's July 2026 lawsuit alleges Hims made cancellation hard, hiding the cancel option behind unchecking each medication and then three to ten survey screens [S19][S21]. This suggests some "retention" was friction, not loyalty. It is now a legal risk (our view; FTC allegations are disputed by Hims [S19][S21]).
- Copyable? There is nothing to copy here; switching costs are low across the industry.

### 1.6 Regulatory licences and partnerships
- Hims operates telehealth across US states, plus UK, Germany, France, Ireland and Spain (Zava), Canada (from May 2026) and Australia (Eucalyptus) [S14][S28][S4][S29].
- The March 2026 Novo collaboration lets Hims sell Wegovy pills and injections and Ozempic [S7][S8]. But Novo also partners with Ro, LifeMD, WeightWatchers and Sesame [S30]. So access to branded drugs is not exclusive.
- The Novo deal includes a clause that Novo can refile its lawsuit; it was dismissed without prejudice [S7]. An earlier 2025 deal ended within about eight weeks [S7][S31]. The partner has shown it can walk away (our view).
- Copyable? Licences take time but not unique skill. Many rivals hold them.

### 1.7 Breadth of categories (a multi-product platform)
- Hims sells hair loss, sexual health, skin, mental health, weight loss, testosterone (from September 2025), menopause (late 2025), labs, and plans longevity in 2026 [S32][S33][S3].
- This is the most plausible durable advantage: one account, one brand, many needs, so the cost to acquire each customer is spread over more products (our view).
- Copyable? Ro has a similar spread. LifeMD too. But few rivals have Hims' scale across this many categories (our view).

**Moat conclusion (our view):** Hims' moat is shallow and mostly scale-based: brand awareness, a big subscriber base, multi-category cross-selling and some in-house supply. The GLP-1 switch from compounded to branded drugs removed its most distinct product. The drug makers, Amazon and Costco now compete for the same patient with the same drug. Trust has been damaged by the FTC and Visa actions.

## 2. Competitors and their numbers

### 2.1 Ro (private)
- Last priced round: $150 million at a $7 billion valuation in February 2022 [S34].
- Nasdaq Private Market showed an implied price of $7.02 per share as of 18 September 2026 (a secondary-market estimate, not a funding round) (unverified) [S35].
- Revenue: Sacra estimates about $598 million annualised in 2024, up 66% from $360 million in 2023, with GLP-1s at about 40% of revenue (about $370 million) by end-2024. These are estimates, not company numbers (unverified) [S34].
- Campaign: Serena Williams joined Ro as GLP-1 ambassador on 21 August 2025 [S5]. Ro's CEO told Bloomberg (1 October 2025) the ad caused a spike in web traffic and new customers [S36]. Ro's first Super Bowl ad, "Healthier on Ro", aired February 2026 and was made in-house [S6][S37].
- GLP-1 business: Novo partnered with Ro on the Wegovy pill in January 2026 [S30][S34]. Ro added Lilly's oral Foundayo on 9 April 2026 (unverified; Tracxn) [S38]. A rival's comparison page says Ro sells only FDA-approved GLP-1s, not compounded (checked 27 September 2026) (unverified) [S39].
- Ro's membership: $39 first month, then $74/month with annual prepay or $149 month-to-month, drug extra (as of 15 August 2026) (unverified) [S39].
- Read-across (our view): Ro chose branded-only earlier. That now looks like the right call. Ro has no public-market pressure and no compounding fight with Novo.

### 2.2 LifeMD (NASDAQ: LFMD)
- Q2 2026 (reported 5 August 2026): revenue $47.3 million; adjusted EBITDA loss about $3.5 million; gross margin about 89% [S12].
- Weight management subscribers about 108,000; total active subscribers about 356,000, up 20% year on year [S12].
- About 95% of new weight patients start on branded GLP-1s [S12].
- FY2026 revenue guidance cut to $205.5–212.5 million [S12].
- Launching XYOSTED (an injectable testosterone) with $2–3 million of net launch costs in 2026 [S12]. That makes it a direct testosterone rival.
- It cited higher CAC and a planned cut in marketing [S12].
- Scale versus Hims: about 1/16th of Hims' quarterly revenue (our calculation from [S1][S12]).

### 2.3 WeightWatchers (post-bankruptcy)
- Filed for bankruptcy in May 2025, cut about $1.15 billion of debt, relisted on Nasdaq, and repositioned around menopause and women's health [S40][S41].
- Q2 2026: revenue $162.3 million, down 14%; Clinical (prescribing) subscribers 197,000, up 55.7%; Clinical subscription revenue $39.9 million, up 30.4% [S42] (via Quartr summary).
- CEO resigned March 2026; an interim office of the CEO runs the company [S42] (unverified detail).
- From 22 May 2026 it began moving Clinical patients from compounded semaglutide to FDA-approved drugs [S42] (unverified detail).
- FY2026 revenue guidance $620–635 million [S42].
- Read-across (our view): WW is a small but fast-growing rival in GLP-1 prescribing and a direct rival in menopause.

### 2.4 Noom (private)
- Sells a "Microdose GLP-1" programme using compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide [S43][S44].
- Price per Noom's page: $79 for the first 3 weeks plus 4 weeks of drug, then $199/month on a 12-week plan, effective 31 March 2026 for new accounts [S44]. Older pages show lower prices (conflicting).
- Read-across (our view): Noom is still in the compounded lane Hims has left. It is exposed to the same FDA risk.

### 2.5 Amazon One Medical
- Launched a national GLP-1 weight-loss programme on 21 April 2026 [S16][S45].
- Pricing: from $25/month with insurance; cash pay from $149/month for oral pills and $299/month for injectables (drug cost) [S16][S45].
- Cash-pay virtual care is in 48 states plus DC, from $39 for message care and $59 for video [S46].
- Walmart launched its own GLP-1 platform days earlier (April 2026) [S16].
- Read-across (our view): Amazon pairs insurance billing, a pharmacy, and Prime-scale reach. Hims is mostly cash-pay. That is a real threat for insured patients.

### 2.6 Costco and Sesame
- Programme launched in 2024 at $179 for three months, drug excluded [S17].
- Current prices from third-party sites conflict: one says Wegovy/Ozempic $349/month self-pay with $199 for the first two fills; another says about $398/month total ($99 Sesame plan plus $299 Wegovy pill) (unverified, conflicting) [S47][S48].
- Sesame was expected to join Novo's subscription programme (Novo Q1 2026) [S30].

### 2.7 GoodRx
- Offers cash prices for the semaglutide pill from $149/month for some doses [S49].
- GoodRx reports 88% of people face insurance restrictions on Wegovy, Zepbound, Foundayo and Saxenda [S50].
- Read-across (our view): GoodRx competes for the price-shopping patient at the point of fill, not with a care programme.

### 2.8 Drug-maker direct channels: LillyDirect, NovoCare, TrumpRx
- LillyDirect: in Q2 2025, about 35% of new Zepbound prescriptions were filled through LillyDirect self-pay [S51]. Self-pay vial tiers cited at $299 (2.5 mg), $399 (5 mg) and $449 (7.5–15 mg, with a refill-timing rule) (unverified, third-party) [S52]. LillyDirect works with independent telehealth prescribers and Walmart pickup (October 2025) [S52][S53].
- NovoCare: Wegovy self-pay standard price cited at $349/month; first two pen fills $199 through 31 December 2026 (unverified, third-party) [S52].
- Novo's Q2 2026 filing: in the week ending 17 July 2026, about 120,000 of about 310,000 weekly US injectable Wegovy prescriptions went through the self-pay channel (retail and NovoCare, including telehealth) [S54]. That is about 39% (our calculation). Oral Wegovy prescriptions passed 3 million since its January 2026 launch [S55].
- TrumpRx: listed Zepbound vials at $299/month and the Wegovy pill at $149/month in September 2026 (unverified, third-party) [S56][S52]. A November 2025 White House agreement set Novo's direct Wegovy price at $350/month in 2026 [S52].
- Read-across (our view): the drug makers now set the price and own the pharmacy. Telehealth firms become prescribing front-ends that earn a membership or visit fee. This caps Hims' margin on GLP-1s.

### 2.9 Function Health and lab-testing players
- Function Health raised a $298 million Series B at a $2.5 billion valuation in November 2025 [S57][S58].
- PitchBook and Tracxn list a $450 million debt round on 30 July 2026 (unverified) [S59].
- Oura and other wearables now offer blood tests (NPR, 14 April 2026) [S60].
- Read-across (our view): Hims Labs (via Trybe) enters a field with a well-funded leader. Function has the premium "longevity" brand. Hims' edge would be price and cross-sell to existing subscribers.

### 2.10 Menopause players: Midi, Alloy
- Midi Health: $100 million Series D at about a $1 billion valuation, led by Goodwater [S61]. Forbes (June 2026) says Midi has raised over $250 million and has a $150 million revenue run rate, up from $60 million at end-2024 [S62][S63].
- Alloy: about $21 million raised in total and described as profitable (Forbes, 22 June 2026) [S62].
- WeightWatchers also targets menopause [S40][S41].
- Read-across (our view): Midi is insurance-billed and clinician-led, which builds trust for a complex condition. Hers competes on price and convenience.

### 2.11 Testosterone clinics
- Hims entered low testosterone in September 2025 with enclomiphene and enclomiphene plus tadalafil, after an at-home blood test [S32][S33]. It announced a collaboration with Marius Pharmaceuticals for a branded oral testosterone [S32].
- LifeMD is launching XYOSTED (2026) [S12].
- Gap: we did not find 2026 data on independent men's clinics (for example Defy, Hone, Maximus) in this run.

### 2.12 Who is gaining share in GLP-1 telehealth?
- No source gives measured 2026 market shares (gap). IQVIA-type prescription data is paywalled.
- Proxies:
  - Drug-maker direct channels are big: about 35% of new Zepbound scripts via LillyDirect in Q2 2025 [S51]; about 39% of injectable Wegovy scripts via self-pay in July 2026 [S54].
  - Hims: management said on the Q1 2026 call it was on track to add over 100,000 new weight-loss subscribers per month after the branded launch; it fulfilled over 125,000 Wegovy shipments within six weeks (unverified, secondary summary) [S3][S64]. US revenue grew 16% year on year in Q2 2026 to about $622 million [S1][S2].
  - WW Clinical subscribers +55.7% year on year to 197,000 [S42]; LifeMD weight subscribers about 108,000 [S12].
- Our view: the winners in 2026 appear to be the drug makers' own channels and large incumbents with insurance and pharmacies (Amazon, Walmart, Costco). Hims remains the largest pure-play telehealth GLP-1 seller by revenue, but at lower margin than in the compounded era.

**What has worked (our view):**
- The fast pivot to branded Wegovy after March 2026. It brought back revenue growth (Q2 revenue about $753m, +38%; FY2026 guide raised to $3.1–3.3bn) [S1][S2].
- International build-out by acquisition. Hims now has scale in UK/EU, Canada and Australia. International was about 17% of Q2 revenue (our calculation from [S1][S2]).
- Super Bowl-scale marketing that drives sign-ups [S11][S23].

**What has not worked (our view):**
- The compounded-GLP-1 strategy. It ended two Novo relationships, triggered an FDA rebuke and a lawsuit, and forced an exit with restructuring costs [S7][S18][S31].
- Margins: gross margin down 12 points year on year [S1][S2].
- Customer practices: FTC/state lawsuit (July 2026) and Visa monitoring (August 2026) [S19][S20].
- New categories (menopause, testosterone, labs, longevity) have no disclosed revenue. We cannot tell if they work (gap).

**Growth options (our view):**
1. Branded GLP-1 volume, including Lilly products if a deal comes (one secondary source says Hims sells Zepbound; unverified) [S64].
2. International: Eucalyptus and Zava as platforms for Europe and Asia-Pacific; generic semaglutide in markets where patents have lapsed (Canada from 2026) [S29].
3. Hormones and longevity: testosterone, menopause, peptides, labs. High margin but high regulatory risk.
4. Insurance: Hims is mostly cash-pay. Amazon and Midi take insurance. Adding insurance would widen reach but cut pricing power.

## (a) Ten-bullet summary

1. Hims' moat is shallow. It rests on brand awareness, scale, and many categories under one account, not on anything rivals cannot copy (our view).
2. The compounded GLP-1 era gave Hims a unique, high-margin product. That is largely gone after the March 2026 Novo deal [S7][S8].
3. In branded GLP-1s, Hims sells the same drug as Ro, Amazon, Costco/Sesame, WW, LifeMD and the drug makers' own channels [S16][S17][S30][S51].
4. Drug-maker direct channels are big: about 35% of new Zepbound scripts via LillyDirect (Q2 2025) and about 39% of injectable Wegovy scripts via self-pay (July 2026) [S51][S54].
5. Growth is back: Q2 2026 revenue about $753m (+38%) and FY guide $3.1–3.3bn, helped by Wegovy and acquisitions [S1][S2].
6. Profitability per order is lower: gross margin fell from 76% to 64% [S1][S2].
7. International (Zava, Eucalyptus, Canada) is now about 17% of revenue and is the clearest new growth leg (our calculation) [S1][S4][S29].
8. Trust is the main brand risk: FTC/state lawsuit (July 2026) and Visa monitoring (August 2026) [S19][S20].
9. Ro is the strongest private rival: $7bn last valuation (2022), Serena Williams campaign, branded-only [S5][S34].
10. Dudum controls about 87% of votes through Class V shares (our calculation; holder not confirmed); insiders are net sellers [S71][S73].
