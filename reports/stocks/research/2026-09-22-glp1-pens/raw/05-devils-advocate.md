## (a) The single strongest bear argument

Lilly and Novo keep the valuable steps in-house: fill-finish (Novo owns the ex-Catalent sites; Lilly has more than $50bn of plants) and pen assembly (FlexTouch, KwikPen, Lilly's Concord site). Listed suppliers sell components. By my rough estimate they share about $2–2.5bn a year of GLP-1 revenue, about 2–3% of the roughly $95bn incretin drug market **[derived]**.

At the same time, the formats that are winning cut the number of devices per patient: pills, 4-dose pens (13 devices a year instead of 52) and monthly shots. Report 04's own base case already has device units per patient-year falling 28% by 2030. In a plausible bear case, 2030 autoinjector volume ends up **below 2026 levels** even though patient numbers grow 60%.

Every supplier sized new capacity during the 2022–24 shortage, and that shortage ended in February 2025. Equipment orders are already falling: ATS bookings -18.4%, Stevanato Engineering -31%, Mikron -6%. A key customer's syringe dispute at Schott points to over-ordering.

The stocks are still priced as long-term compounders: West at about 42x FY26 EPS, Stevanato about 28x forward with free cash flow near zero. That is the same set-up that took West down 56% and Stevanato 44% from their post-COVID peaks.

## Worst plausible 2030 device count vs the Report 04 base

Assumptions are mine **[reasoning]**. The arithmetic follows Report 04's units per patient-year.

| | 2026 (R04) | 2030 base (R04: 70M patient-years; oral 25%, monthly 3%, weekly autoinjector 20%, 4-dose pen 46%) | 2030 moderate bear (60M; oral 35%, monthly 5%, autoinjector 14%, pen 42%, vials 3%, daily 1%) | **2030 worst plausible (50M; oral 40%, monthly 6%, autoinjector 10%, pen 40%, vials 3%, daily 1%)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Autoinjectors / prefilled syringes (weekly + monthly) | 463M | 753M | 473M (-37% vs base; flat vs 2026) | **296M (-61% vs base; -36% vs 2026)** |
| Pens / cartridges | 271M | 475M | 352M (-26%) | **280M (-41%; about flat vs 2026)** |
| Pen needles | 1,494M | 2,185M | 1,529M (-30%) | **1,223M (-44%; -18% vs 2026)** |
| Vials | 82M | 107M | ~68M | **~57M** |
| All delivery units (autoinjectors + pens + vials) | 816M | 1,335M | ~893M | **~633M (-53% vs base; -22% vs 2026)** |

**Why these assumptions are not extreme:**
- 50M patient-years is Report 04's own low end.
- The Wegovy pill is already about 35% of Wegovy prescriptions after 8 months.
- The Medicare Bridge covers only the Zepbound KwikPen, and the KwikPen is priced the same as the vial.
- A US Wegovy multi-dose FlexTouch is reportedly launching.
- Pfizer registered VESPER-6, a Phase 3 trial of its once-monthly GLP-1, on 19 May 2026.
- 64.8% of obesity-only patients stop within one year.

**The weak point in the base case:** it assumes US weekly single-dose autoinjector patients grow from 8.9M to 14.0M (+57%), while every US pricing and channel signal points toward 4-dose pens.

## (b) Ranked attacks

**1. Format tax: devices per patient collapse in the formats that matter. Severity: HIGH.**
- **Claim:** growth in patients does not become growth in autoinjectors.
- **Evidence:**
  - Zepbound KwikPen (4 doses) was approved on 23 Feb 2026 at $299–449/month, the same as vials.
  - The Medicare GLP-1 Bridge ($50 copay, 1 Jul 2026 to 31 Dec 2027, more than 500k enrolled) covers **only the KwikPen**.
  - A US Wegovy FlexTouch multi-dose pen is FDA-labelled; the launch report comes from a Substack.
  - The Wegovy pill has more than 5M cumulative prescriptions and more than 265k a week (Novo, July 2026).
  - Goldman sees a $22bn oral market by 2030, with Lilly's pill taking about 60%.
  - Pfizer's once-monthly PF'3944 is in Phase 3 (VESPER-6); MariTide Phase 3 reads out in early 2027.
  - Each 1M patients who move from a weekly autoinjector to a 4-dose pen removes 39M autoinjectors (Report 04).
  - Autoinjector volume in my worst case: 296M vs the base 753M.
- **Hits hardest:** Ypsomed, SHL (private), BD (Neopak/Intevia), Stevanato and Schott (1 mL syringes), and West (fewer plungers per patient). It also hurts ATS and Mikron, because fewer autoinjector lines are needed.
- **Partial offset:** cartridges and pen needles.

**2. Captive profit pool: listed suppliers get components only. Severity: HIGH.**
- **Claim:** most of the device and fill-finish value is kept by Lilly and Novo or goes to private companies.
- **Evidence, captive capacity:**
  - Novo bought the Anagni, Brussels and Bloomington fill-finish sites for $11bn (Dec 2024), taking it from 11 to 14 fill-finish sites. It also has a $4.1bn Clayton NC plant.
  - FlexTouch is designed and assembled by Novo.
  - Lilly has Concord NC device assembly and more than $50bn of US plants. The Resilience KwikPen line (private, $750M, early 2027) and the Lehigh Valley plant (operational 2031) add more.
- **Evidence, listed revenue [derived]:**
  - West: about 18% of roughly $3.36bn sales, about $605M.
  - Stevanato: about 22.5% of €1.27bn, about €285M.
  - Schott Pharma: about €1bn of contracts to 2030, about €140M a year.
  - Gerresheimer: about €200M target.
  - Datwyler: "low double-digit millions".
  - Embecta: at least $100M a year by 2033.
  - The total is about $2–2.5bn, against Lilly's about $59bn annualised incretin sales (Q2 Mounjaro $9.9bn plus Zepbound $4.9bn) plus Novo's semaglutide.
  - That is about $2.70 of listed-supplier revenue per 2026 delivery unit **[derived]**.
- **Hits hardest:** anyone selling assembly or fill-finish capacity: Jabil, West's contract manufacturing arm (West Vantage), ATS, Mikron and the CDMOs.

**3. Overbuild and bullwhip: the 2021 COVID-vial pattern again. Severity: HIGH.**
- **Claim:** capacity ordered during the shortage arrives after the shortage has ended.
- **Evidence, shortage over:** FDA declared the tirzepatide shortage resolved on 19 Dec 2024 and semaglutide on 21 Feb 2025.
- **Evidence, capacity landing 2026–28:**
  - BD: $110M Neopak syringe line (mid-2026).
  - Stevanato: Fishers (late 2026/27) and the Latina RTU 400 cartridge line (2027).
  - Nipro: more than $60M of tubing capacity plus a $398M NC plant (2bn units).
  - Ypsomed: CHF 1.5bn of capex over four years; Holly Springs from end-2027.
  - SHL: $220M SC plant. Jabil: a new GLP-1 site in FY27. Gerresheimer: $180M Peachtree City.
  - CordenPharma: 100M autoinjectors a year for Viking. Vetter: €2bn. Simtra: $250M.
- **Evidence, cycle already turning:**
  - ATS: Q4 FY26 bookings -18.4%, backlog -8.5%, Q1 FY27 revenue -5.2%, net debt/EBITDA 2.9x.
  - Mikron: H1 2026 sales -6%; the large 2024–25 GLP-1 orders are still being installed.
  - Stevanato Engineering: Q1 2026 revenue -31%.
  - Schott: in Dec 2025 it guided lower on "the revised market outlook of a key customer resulting in lower glass syringes demand". The dispute was settled as take-or-pay, with a one-off €15M in Q4 FY26.
  - medmix: Drug Delivery fell because a customer moved to dual sourcing.
  - Novo: capex falls from DKK 60bn to about 55bn and is "expected to decline".
- **Base rate:** Schott planned about 40,000 t of extra tubing, about 7bn containers, for COVID. Stevanato and West were then hit by destocking in 2023–24.
- **Hits hardest:** Stevanato, Schott Pharma, ATS, Mikron, Gerresheimer, Nipro.

**4. Valuation leaves no room for growth halving. Severity: HIGH.**
- **West:** $373.56, about 42x the FY26 EPS midpoint ($8.95). At 30x, roughly its multiple at the July 2025 trough, it would be $268 (-28%). At 25x, $224 (-40%) **[derived]**.
- **Stevanato:** $19.67 after Q2, about 28x the forward adjusted EPS midpoint (€0.61 × 1.16), about 31x trailing. Q2 free cash flow was -€32M on €52M of capex; FY free cash flow is guided at breakeven to €20M, a yield of about 0.3%. At 20x it would be $14.2 (-28%) **[derived]**.
- **Ypsomed:** CHF 351.20 (16 Sep 2026), about 22x FY26/27 guided EBIT. The mid-term target of CHF 0.9–1.1bn sales implies an 11–16% CAGR from CHF 601.5M. GLP-1s are about one-third of growth. If that contribution halves, the target falls to about CHF 0.85–1.0bn, and a de-rating to 15x EBIT would mean about -30% **[derived/reasoning]**.
- **Schott Pharma:** €23.65, near its 52-week high of €23.85 and up 87% from the €12.62 low. About 22x P/E, and that includes a non-recurring €15M. Back at July's 16.6x it would be about €17.8 (-25%) **[derived]**.
- **Datwyler:** 34x P/E on GLP-1 revenue in the low double-digit millions.
- **Hits hardest:** West, Stevanato, Ypsomed, Datwyler, and Schott after its rally.

**5. The link from supplier to drug is unproven. Severity: MEDIUM-HIGH.**
- **Claim:** the bull thesis assumes supplier X is in drug Y. The team's own reports could not confirm this.
- **Evidence:**
  - Report 01: "no source ties a named vendor to the Mounjaro, Zepbound, Ozempic or Wegovy cartridge, syringe or plunger."
  - That West's contract manufacturing work is for Lilly autoinjectors, and that BD supplies Lilly's syringe, is market lore.
  - Every patient-year split in Report 04 is an estimate derived from revenue. Lilly and Novo do not disclose devices shipped.
- **Hits hardest:** West, BD, Datwyler, Stevanato.

**6. Concentration, plus the weakest originator. Severity: MEDIUM-HIGH.**
- **Evidence:**
  - Ypsomed is tied to Novo through a co-funded autoinjector deal. Novo guides 2026 adjusted sales at 0% to -6% CER, Wegovy injectable -22%, Ozempic/Rybelsus -16%, about DKK 8bn of restructuring, and falling capex.
  - Stevanato's GLP-1 share (22–23%) comes from "two big originators".
  - Schott depends on one key syringe customer, as the dispute showed.
  - Datwyler supplies one "leading GLP-1".
  - West's GLP-1 contract manufacturing is 8% of sales, low-margin (4Q25 gross margin 12.7%, operating margin 6.8%) and grew only +2% in Q2 2026.
- **Hits hardest:** Ypsomed (the CagriSema decision is due around late 2026), Schott, Datwyler, West's contract manufacturing arm.

**7. Reimbursement and persistence cap patient-years. Severity: MEDIUM.**
- **Discontinuation:** 64.8% of obesity-only patients stop GLP-1s within one year, and 46.5% of those with diabetes. Published ranges run from 37% to 81%. Only 36.3% restart within a year.
- **Employer coverage:**
  - Employers covering GLP-1s for weight loss fell from 72% (2025) to 60% (2026).
  - 14% have dropped coverage or plan to by 2027.
  - GLP-1s are now 11.4% of employer claims, up from 6.9% in 2023.
  - Starbucks stops coverage in October 2026.
- **Medicare cliff:** the Medicare Bridge **expires 31 Dec 2027**. That is a potential volume cliff in 2028 unless it is extended.
- **Hits hardest:** all of them, most of all the high-multiple names.

**8. Price deflation and commoditisation reach the suppliers. Severity: MEDIUM.**
- **Evidence:**
  - Wegovy injectable -22% CER; Lilly US price -9% excluding rebate adjustments.
  - Medicare is set at $245/month, and TrumpRx moves from $350 to $245.
  - Generics in India start at INR 1,800/month, about $20 **[reasoning: approximate FX]**, with more than 40 firms. Biocon deferred its India launch because the market is "saturated".
  - Generic pens go to low-cost OEMs: Shaily (unverified), Hangzhou Sunrise, Linuo and Hualan.
  - medmix lost volume to dual sourcing. Embecta's US pen-needle sales fell 24.6%.
- **Honest caveat:** the device is a small share of a $245/month price, so direct pass-through is limited. The pressure shows up through procurement leverage and a mix shift toward low-price generic pens.
- **Hits hardest:** medmix, Stevanato (Alina), BD (Vystra), Embecta.

**9. Base rate: "picks and shovels" for a hot drug class have round-tripped. Severity: MEDIUM.**
- **West:** all-time high $475.35 on 9 Sep 2021, then a 52-week low of $206.80 on 18 Jul 2025 (-56%). 2024 guidance was cut from $3.0–3.025bn to $2.87–2.90bn in sales and from $7.63–7.88 to $6.35–6.65 in EPS, blamed on destocking. The stock fell almost 40% in Feb 2025.
- **Catalent:** it expected COVID revenue to fall about $750M from about $1.3bn in FY22. The shares fell more than 20% to $45.20 on a 2023 guidance cut, from a peak of about $140 in 2021 **[peak: memory, unverified]**. The company was then taken private.
- **Stevanato:** all-time closing high $35.29 (8 Aug 2023); now $19.67 (-44%), below its 2021 IPO price of about $21 **[IPO price: memory]**.
- **Schott Pharma:** still below its 2023 IPO price of about €27 **[memory]**.
- **Caveat:** COVID demand was a one-off and GLP-1 demand is chronic. But the mechanism is the same: double-ordering during a shortage, then destocking.
- **Hits hardest:** Stevanato, Schott, West.

**10. Safety signals. Severity: LOW, for now.**
- **Evidence:**
  - EMA's PRAC classed NAION (an optic-nerve condition that can cause vision loss) as a "very rare" semaglutide side effect, affecting up to 1 in 10,000, with about a two-fold relative risk (June 2025). The UK MHRA added it to labels on 5 Feb 2026.
  - The MHRA warned of rare severe pancreatitis, including necrotising and fatal cases: 1,296 Yellow Card reports from 2007 to October 2025.
  - Muscle loss: I did not search it this session **[reasoning: a behavioural and persistence risk, not yet a regulatory one]**.
- **Assessment:** these do not break the volume thesis on their own. They add to the persistence problem in attack 7.
- **Hits:** all names equally.

## (d) What the bull case gets right

- **Volume is real and accelerating.** US obesity prescriptions are +78% year on year, Lilly revenue is $23.0bn (+48%), and the Medicare Bridge has more than 500k enrollees. Device demand follows volume, not drug revenue.
- **Regulatory lock-in is real for elastomers and containers.** Changing a container needs FDA prior approval, and requalifying a supplier reportedly takes 18–30 months. West's pricing power shows: EPS +28.8% on sales +13.8%.
- **The 4-dose pen shift helps cartridges and pen needles.** It helps cartridge makers (Schott, Stevanato EZ-fill, Nipro) and needle makers even as it hurts autoinjectors. Not every supplier loses.
- **Chronic therapy is not a one-off like COVID.** Even my worst case keeps injectable patient-years roughly flat versus 2026. It does not collapse.
- **The best names have other growth drivers.** West has non-GLP-1 high-value products growing high-teens plus EU Annex 1 upgrades; BD and Aptar are diversified. For them, GLP-1 disappointment means a lower multiple, not a solvency problem.

## (e) Verdicts

**Core names:**
- **West (WST):** only on a big drawdown. Best moat, but 42x prices in perfection; the attractive level is the 25–30x zone of $224–268.
- **Stevanato (STVN):** avoid. The most exposed to overbuild and the syringe-to-cartridge shift, with free cash flow near zero at about 28x.
- **Schott Pharma (1SXP):** only on a big drawdown. It has rallied 87% off the low into a one-off-flattered FY26, with one-customer risk.
- **Ypsomed (YPSN):** only on a big drawdown. It is the real scarce layer, but tied to Novo, the weaker originator, and to autoinjectors, the losing format.
- **Gerresheimer (GXI):** avoid. Refinancing, BaFin and negative free cash flow come first; GLP-1 is irrelevant until those are solved.
- **BD (BDX):** survives the attack, because GLP-1 is too small to matter to BD. It is not a GLP-1 play.
- **Aptar (ATR):** survives the attack. It is diversified at 22.5x, but margins are compressing and it is a weak GLP-1 proxy.
- **Datwyler (DAE):** avoid for this thesis. 34x P/E on a single small GLP-1 customer.
- **Embecta (EMBC):** only on a big drawdown. 2.8x EPS reflects US decline and leverage; the generic co-pack is an option, not a thesis.

**Equipment and contract manufacturers:**
- **ATS (ATS.TO):** avoid. The GLP-1 capex cycle has rolled over, with leverage of 2.9x.
- **Mikron (MIKN):** avoid. The same cycle, and smaller.
- **Jabil (JBL) and Flex (FLEX):** survive the attack. GLP-1 is immaterial next to AI and cloud revenue; they are not GLP-1 plays.
- **medmix (MEDX):** avoid. Its drug-delivery business is already losing to dual sourcing.

**Materials and peptide suppliers:**
- **Nipro (8086.T):** only on a big drawdown. There was not enough data; it is a cartridge-mix winner but has capacity-glut risk.
- **Bachem (BANB):** only on a big drawdown. Execution risk on H2, and orforglipron needs no peptide API.
- **PolyPeptide (PPGN):** not a GLP-1 bet any more. It is merger arbitrage at CHF 44.31.
- **Lonza, Siegfried, Samsung Biologics, Thermo Fisher:** survive the attack. GLP-1 is not their thesis.
- **WuXi AppTec (TIDES unit), Gland Pharma, Asymchem:** only on a big drawdown. Generic peptide demand is real, but there is geopolitical and price risk.
- **Corning, Celanese, Covestro, DuPont, Zeon, NEG, Terumo, Steris, Danaher, Medtronic:** survive the attack. GLP-1 is immaterial to each; there is no thesis here.
- **Novo (NVO) and Lilly (LLY):** outside the supplier thesis. They capture the device value themselves, which is the point of attack 2.
