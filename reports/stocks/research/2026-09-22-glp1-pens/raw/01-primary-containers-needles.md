**Regulatory switching costs, which apply to every row:**
- The approved label names the container materials, not the vendors. Ozempic's EU SmPC specifies a "1.5 mL or 3 mL glass cartridge (Type I glass) closed... with a rubber plunger (chlorobutyl)... aluminium cap with a rubber disc (bromobutyl/polyisoprene)".
- FDA treats a change to a prefilled-syringe container as "major", requiring a Prior Approval Supplement. The filing needs about 3 months of comparative accelerated stability data plus long-term data on at least 1 to 3 batches.
- Requalifying a stopper supplier reportedly takes 18 to 30 months (secondary source, unverified).
- Lock-in is therefore strongest for elastomers, because they are in contact with the drug and involve formulation-specific extractables and leachables work, and for the syringe-plus-autoinjector fit.

## (b) Detail by supplier

### Elastomers

**West Pharmaceutical (WST): strongest moat in the chain**
- **Q2 2026 (reported 23 Jul 2026):**
  - Revenue $872.3M, +13.8% (+13% organic). Adjusted EPS $2.37, +28.8%.
  - High-value product (HVP) components $424M, 49% of revenue (46% a year earlier).
- **GLP-1 exposure:**
  - Q2 2026: GLP-1 elastomers were 10% of total revenue and GLP-1 contract manufacturing (West Vantage) another 8%, so about 18% in total. That is roughly $157M a quarter (derived).
  - Q4 2025: 10% elastomers plus 7% contract manufacturing. Q1 2026: 18% in total, of which 8% contract manufacturing.
  - Management expects both GLP-1 and non-GLP-1 HVP components to grow in the high teens.
- **FY2026 guidance (raised):** revenue $3.345–3.38B, adjusted EPS $8.85–9.05.
- **Other drivers and headwinds:**
  - EU Annex 1 upgrades add about 200bp of 2026 growth; roughly 800 projects are in hand, +50% year on year.
  - West Vantage (contract manufacturing) sales were $149.7M, +2%. Management is pivoting it toward drug handling.
  - Exit of a legacy CGM contract takes about $40M out of 2H26 revenue.
  - A ransomware attack on 4–7 May 2026 cost about $7M of Q2 revenue.
- **Valuation:** $373.56 per share and about $26.3B market cap on 22 Sep 2026. Trailing P/E about 46x; about 42x the FY26 EPS midpoint (derived). EV/EBITDA: MISSING.
- **Moat evidence:** An often-cited but unverified estimate gives West about 70% of injectable elastomer components. Its elastomers are specified into drug filings (18–30 months to requalify).
- **Biggest risks:**
  - Valuation.
  - GLP-1 contract manufacturing is lower-margin and tied to a single customer. It is widely assumed to be Lilly autoinjector parts, but that is unverified.
  - A shift to oral drugs (see below).
  - Cyber and operational risk.

**Aptar Pharma (ATR)**
- Q2 2026: Pharma segment $458M, +4% reported / +1% core. Excluding emergency-medicine destocking, Pharma core growth was high single digit. Injectables core +9%, on elastomers for GLP-1, biologics and vaccines. Group adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 20.7% from 22.6%.
- 2025: injectables elastomerics +18% in Q3 and +24% in Q4. GLP-1 sales were up more than 40% year to date (secondary source).
- Product fit: PremiumCoat ETFE-coated plungers with a 3-rib design are marketed for GLP-1 autoinjector integration.
- Valuation: about $7.9–8.0B market cap, P/E about 22.5x (Sep 2026).
- Risk: Pharma is diluted by the consumer and beauty segments, and margins are compressing.

**Datwyler (DAE.SW)**
- H1 2026:
  - Group revenue CHF 582.7M, 4.6% organic growth.
  - Healthcare revenue CHF 242.8M (from 236.8M). Healthcare EBIT CHF 50.8M, margin 20.9% (from 16.9%), helped by a one-off from realigning the production network.
- GLP-1:
  - Began supplying components for "a leading GLP-1 weight-loss medication" from its FirstLine site in Middletown, USA.
  - GLP-1 revenue will be only "low double-digit millions" in 2026. It is ahead of plan, with volumes +60–80% in 2026 and more programmes due in 2027–28.
- Valuation: CHF 126.60 per share, about CHF 2B market cap, P/E about 34x.
- Risk: GLP-1 is still sub-scale, and the Industrial division is a drag.

**Chinese entrants**
- Jiangsu Hualan New Pharmaceutical Material (301093.SZ) makes about 10bn stoppers a year and holds Chinese registration for coated stoppers, including prefilled-syringe and cartridge elastomers.
- It is positioned for China's semaglutide generics: the compound patent expired on 20 Mar 2026, and Qilu and Bcht approvals were expected as early as Q3 2026.
- I found no evidence that any Chinese stopper maker supplies Western GLP-1 programmes.

### Glass cartridges and syringes

**Stevanato Group (STVN)**
- Q2 2026 (reported 4 Aug 2026):
  - Revenue €302.0M, +8%. BDS (biopharma and diagnostic solutions) segment €266.2M, +9%.
  - High-value solutions €135.9M, +16%, which is 45% of group and 51% of BDS revenue. Growth came mostly from high-performance syringes.
  - Adjusted EBITDA margin about 26%. Biologics revenue +30%.
- GLP-1 share of revenue: about 21–22% in Q1 2026 and 22–23% by mid-2026. Implied GLP-1 revenue for FY26 is about €280–295M (derived).
- Q1 2026: high-value syringes grew more than 20%.
- FY2026 guidance (narrowed in August):
  - Revenue €1.260–1.280B (from €1.260–1.290B, reflecting the divestiture of Balda C. Brewer).
  - Adjusted EBITDA €335–345.2M. Adjusted EPS €0.60–0.62.
  - High-value solutions 47–48% of sales.
- Capacity:
  - Fishers, Indiana (US hub) starts commercial production in late 2026 or early 2027.
  - Latina, Italy adds prefillable syringes and EZ-fill cartridges.
  - A vial line at Piombino Dese is being converted to cartridges to bridge near-term cartridge tightness (secondary source).
- Device platforms: the Alina pen won EU approvals for liraglutide generics, and the Deora fixed-dose pen has launched.
- Moat claim: describes itself as "market leader in cartridge systems" (self-described).
- Valuation: about $5.7–5.9B market cap, EV about $6.1B, trailing P/E about 31x (1 Sep 2026). Forward EV/EBITDA is about 15–16x assuming roughly 1.16 USD/EUR (derived).
- Biggest risks:
  - Heavy capex and negative free cash flow (the shares fell after Q2).
  - About 22% GLP-1 concentration.
  - Syringe oversupply in 2027–28.

**Schott Pharma (1SXP.DE), fiscal year ends September**
- FY2025: revenue €986.2M (+5.8% at constant currency), EBITDA €280.3M (28.4% margin).
  - Drug Containment Solutions (DCS, cartridges and vials) €548.0M, +11.9%, led by sterile ready-to-use cartridges.
  - Drug Delivery Systems (DDS, syringes) €438.8M, -1.3%: polymer syringes were weak and glass syringes strong.
- Q3 FY2026:
  - Revenue €282M, +10% (+8.3% at constant currency). EBITDA margin 26.8%. High-value solutions were 59% of revenue.
  - DDS €123.2M (from €113.5M), recovering on GLP-1 glass syringes.
  - DCS 9-month revenue €445.3M, +9.2% at constant currency.
- FY2026 guidance raised: +5–6% at constant currency (from 2–5%), EBITDA margin 27–28% (from about 27%).
- Customer dispute and settlement:
  - A dispute with a key glass-syringe customer had triggered a December 2025 profit warning.
  - It was settled as a take-or-pay agreement for coming years, with a one-off contribution of about €15M in Q4 FY26 that will not recur in 2027.
- GLP-1 contracts: about €1B of GLP-1 cartridge and syringe contracts running to 2030 (disclosed at the 2023 IPO).
- Other: a collaboration with Nemera on 3 mL ready-to-use cartridge and pen compatibility. 2027 capex will be above 2026, then taper.
- Valuation: €23.65 per share on 22 Sep 2026 (52-week high €23.85, low €12.62). At about 150.6M shares, market cap is about €3.5B (derived; an earlier €2.2B figure is stale).
  - About 8.6x EV/EBITDA and 16.6x P/E at €17.46 in July. At the current price that implies roughly 11–12x EV/EBITDA and about 22x P/E (derived).
  - Consensus target about €24.1. JPMorgan initiated at Overweight, target €27.10, on 21 Sep 2026.
- Biggest risks: the customer dispute showed syringe demand is volatile. Majority control by Schott AG and Carl Zeiss Foundation governance. Polymer syringe weakness.

**Gerresheimer (GXI.DE): the distressed name**
- **Reporting and accounting:**
  - The FY2025 report was delayed to 29 Jun 2026. It showed a net loss of €318.7M, after about €521.5M of depreciation, amortisation and impairments (Sensile Medical, goodwill, the Chicago moulded-glass plant).
  - BaFin found indications of accounting violations: lease liabilities misstated by €65.5M, capitalised development-cost disclosures, unrecognised impairments in Advanced Technologies (book value about €197M), and bill-and-hold revenue in the H1 2025 report.
- **Guidance cut (29 Jun 2026):** revenue in the lower half of €2.3–2.4B, adjusted EBITDA margin about 17–18% (from 18–19%), free cash flow -€50M to -€100M.
- **Q1 2026 (published August):** revenue €524M, adjusted EBITDA €66M. The new Primary Injectable Solutions division (syringes and tubular glass) had revenue of €101M.
- **Balance sheet:**
  - About €870M of promissory notes must be refinanced by 30 Sep 2026; 96% of holders agreed to the deadline extension.
  - The leverage covenant is 4.75x EBITDA.
  - Centor and Primary Packaging Plastics are being sold to Apax for about €1.5B: Centor closes by end-2026, the plastics unit in H1 2027.
- **Takeover interest:** rejected a non-binding Silgan approach at about €41 a share.
- **Valuation:** €26.32 per share, about €1.0B market cap (September 2026). EV/EBITDA: MISSING (net debt not confirmed).
- **GLP-1 exposure:** earlier management said GLP-1 sales would exceed €100M in 2024 and reach about €350M within three years. It makes pen bodies (devices, not primary containers) for both Novo and Lilly. Short-seller Morpheus flagged the CagriSema dual-chamber syringe as an at-risk growth driver.
- **Biggest risks:** refinancing and the covenant, the BaFin outcome, and customer confidence in it as a primary-container supplier.

**BD (BDX)**
- Fiscal Q3 2026: BioPharma Systems revenue $670M, +6.6% reported / +5.2% currency-neutral.
- About 100 agreements signed across novel and biosimilar GLP-1 programmes. Brazil deal with EMS for semaglutide on BD's Vystra pen.
- Capacity: $110M investment (Jan 2026) to bring Neopak glass syringe production to Columbus, Nebraska; supply starts mid-2026. Neopak comes in 1 mL and 2.25 mL.
- FY26 adjusted EPS guidance midpoint $12.67.
- Company structure: the Biosciences and Diagnostics business was spun into Waters on 9 Feb 2026 (BD received $4B cash).
- Valuation: market cap $44–52B (sources conflict); GAAP P/E about 25x.
- Risk: BioPharma Systems is a small part of BD, and growth is only mid-single digit despite GLP-1.

**Nipro (8086.T)**
- Vertically integrated from glass tubing to cartridges.
- Capacity: more than $60M on tube-draw capacity in France and the US; a Millville NJ line doubled output. New pen and dental cartridge lines in Pune, India. About $398M for a North Carolina plant adding 2bn units (the product scope is unclear).
- Group trailing 12-month revenue about $4.38B (to Mar 2026).
- Pharma-packaging segment financials and valuation: MISSING.

**Corning (GLW)**
- Valor glass (vials, delamination-resistant) and Velocity coated vials.
- The tubing JV with SGD Pharma in Telangana, India opened in September 2025.
- I found no evidence of Valor in any GLP-1 cartridge or syringe (MISSING / likely immaterial).

**SGD Pharma (private)** and **Nippon Electric Glass (5214.T)**: no GLP-1-specific data found (MISSING).

**Linuo (China)**: 13,000 t a year of tubing produced in-house and more than 1.6bn units of capacity, including pen cartridges. Positioned for China's semaglutide generics.

### Polymer (COP/COC)
- Zeon's fifth line lifts ZEONEX/ZEONOR capacity to 54,000 t a year by 2028.
- Market reports cite a shift to COP for high-viscosity biologics.
- Schott reports weak polymer-syringe demand, tied mainly to mRNA vaccines.
- Terumo PLAJEX offers silicone-oil-free polymer syringes with staked needles.
- **I found no approved GLP-1 pen or autoinjector that uses a COP/COC primary container.** The labels checked (Ozempic, Wegovy) specify Type I glass.

### Pen needles
- **Embecta (EMBC):**
  - Fiscal Q3 2026 revenue $271.7M, -8.1%. Pen needles $177.5M, -18.2%. US -24.6%, international +11.5%.
  - FY26 guidance: revenue $1,015–1,035M; adjusted operating margin 23.5–24% and adjusted EPS $1.80–1.90 (both raised).
  - Acquired Owen Mumford (up to £150M; it contributed $13.8M in the quarter).
  - Its needles are co-packaged with generic GLP-1s in India, Canada, Brazil and South Africa; a US small-pack launch is planned for fiscal Q4.
  - Valuation: about $5.19 per share, about $0.3B market cap, about 2.8x forward EPS (derived). Net debt is heavy; exact figure MISSING.
  - Risk: structural decline in US volume and price, and leverage.
- **Ypsomed** sold its pen-needle and blood-glucose business to MTD (March 2026). It is now a pure autoinjector and pen company: commercial autoinjector sales +33.6%, with capacity in Schwerin, Solothurn, Changzhou and Holly Springs.
- **Market size:** the pen-needle market is about $1.8B (2024) growing to about $3.1B by 2030 (market-research estimate).

## (c) Unverified or uncertain claims
- **Supplier-to-drug mapping:** no source ties a named vendor to the Mounjaro, Zepbound, Ozempic or Wegovy cartridge, syringe or plunger. Market lore that BD supplies the syringe in Lilly's autoinjector, and that West's GLP-1 contract manufacturing is Lilly autoinjector parts, is unconfirmed. So is Datwyler's "leading GLP-1" customer.
- West's ~70% elastomer share and the 18–30 month requalification figure come from secondary sources (Substack, pharmabottlenecks).
- The claim that cartridge "shortages [are] expected for at least two years" and the Piombino Dese conversion come from secondary sources.
- Several market-data figures conflict and should be treated as approximate: BDX price and market cap, Embecta P/E, and Schott's €2.2B market cap (stale).
- My recollection that Metsera was acquired by Pfizer in late 2025 was not verified in this session.
- **MISSING entirely:**
  - Devices for MariTide, retatrutide, Viking, Metsera, Structure, and Roche/Zealand.
  - Financials for Terumo, Nipro segment, Zeon, Mitsui Chemicals, Sumitomo Rubber and NEG.
  - Lead-time figures in weeks.
  - Cartridge and syringe pricing.
  - Gerresheimer net debt.

**Key thesis risks for the whole chain:**
- Oral GLP-1s: Novo's oral Wegovy was approved in December 2025 and Lilly's orforglipron (Foundayo) on 1 Apr 2026.
- Pricing pressure as multi-dose pens take over (a Zepbound KwikPen covers a month and uses one cartridge instead of four syringes, which is a mix shift toward cartridges).
- A capacity wave in 2026–28 from BD, Stevanato, Schott and Nipro.
