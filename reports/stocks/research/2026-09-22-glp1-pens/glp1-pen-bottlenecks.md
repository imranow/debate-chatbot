# GLP-1 Injection Pens: Where the Bottleneck Is, and Whether It Pays

*Research date 22 September 2026. Four research streams (primary containers and needles; pens and autoinjectors; fill-finish, equipment and peptide API; demand and device count) plus a devil's-advocate review. Raw reports with sources are in `raw/`. All figures are from web-search snippets because finance sites are blocked from this sandbox; verify against filings before acting. Not investment advice.*

---

## 1. The answer first

The thesis is right that far more people will be on GLP-1 drugs: US obesity prescriptions are up 78% a year, and the demand stream models about 31 million treated patient-years in 2026 rising to about 70 million by 2030. It is wrong that this makes pen suppliers a clean way to own it. Three facts get in the way.

1. **The bottleneck has already moved.** In 2023 to 2024 the binding constraint was sterile fill-finish and pen assembly. FDA declared the tirzepatide shortage over in December 2024 and semaglutide in February 2025. Lilly and Novo solved it by building and buying their own plants (Novo's $11bn purchase of three Catalent fill sites; Lilly's $50bn+ of US plants). The scarce layer in 2026 is peptide drug substance for generics and biotechs, not pens.
2. **The winning formats need fewer devices per patient.** Pills (the Wegovy pill is already about 35% of Wegovy prescriptions), four-dose pens (13 devices a year instead of 52; the Medicare $50 program covers only the Zepbound four-dose KwikPen) and monthly shots (Pfizer and Amgen in Phase 3) all cut units. The demand model has devices per patient-year falling 28% by 2030 in the base case. In the skeptic's worst plausible case, 2030 autoinjector volume ends up 36% below today even as patients grow.
3. **Most of the value is captive.** Lilly and Novo design and assemble their own pens and own their fill lines. Listed suppliers share roughly $2 to 2.5bn a year of GLP-1 revenue, about 2 to 3% of the drug market's value.

**Where a real moat remains:** coated rubber plungers and seals (West), cartridges for the four-dose pens (Schott, Stevanato, Nipro), and outsourced autoinjector platforms for the next wave of drugs from companies without their own device (Ypsomed, private SHL). Of these, only West combines a durable moat with evidence of pricing power, and at about 42 times earnings it is priced for perfection.

## 2. How a GLP-1 pen is made, and who makes each part

| Layer | What it is | Who supplies it | Moat | Bottleneck today? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Peptide drug substance | Tirzepatide / semaglutide made by solid-phase synthesis and purification | Lilly and Novo in-house; Bachem, PolyPeptide (being bought by Samsung Biologics), CordenPharma, WuXi TIDES, Asymchem | Medium-high (2 to 4 years to add qualified capacity) | Yes, for generics and biotechs; the Wegovy pill uses about 73 times more drug per week than the injection |
| Sterile fill-finish | Filling cartridges or syringes in aseptic lines | Mostly Lilly and Novo in-house; CDMOs Vetter, Simtra, Resilience, Jubilant, Samsung, Gland | High (3 to 5 years for a new sterile site) | Was the 2023-24 bottleneck; now easing, overbuild risk by 2028 |
| Filling and assembly machines | Aseptic fill lines, pen assembly automation | Syntegon, Groninger, Optima, IMA (all private); ATS, Mikron listed | Medium, cyclical | No. Orders falling: ATS bookings -18%, Stevanato Engineering -31%, Mikron -6% |
| Glass cartridges (3 mL, four-dose pens) | Type I borosilicate cartridges, ready-to-fill | Stevanato, Schott Pharma, Nipro, Gerresheimer | Medium (named in the drug filing) | Tight for sterile ready-to-fill cartridges as the market shifts to four-dose pens |

| Pen needles | Single-use needles for multi-dose pens | Embecta, Novo (captive), B. Braun, HTL-Strefa | Weak | No. Commoditised; Embecta US -25% |
| Resins | Polycarbonate and acetal plastics | Celanese, Covestro, DuPont, BASF | Low | No; GLP-1 is a tiny share |

## 3. The device-count reality check

| | 2026 | 2030 base | 2030 worst plausible |
|---|---|---|---|
| Treated patient-years | 31M | 70M | 50M |
| Share oral | 8% | 25% | 40% |
| Single-dose autoinjectors (and the syringes inside) | 463M | 753M | 296M |
| Four-dose pens / cartridges | 271M | 475M | 280M |
| Pen needles | 1.49bn | 2.19bn | 1.22bn |
| Devices per patient-year | 26.5 | 19.1 | about 12.7 |

First principles: each million patients who move from a weekly single-dose autoinjector to a four-dose pen removes 39 million autoinjectors and adds 13 million pens and 52 million needles. Each million who move to a monthly shot removes 40 million devices. Each million who move to a pill removes all of them. Supplier revenue therefore tracks the format mix at least as much as patient count, and every current US pricing and channel signal points toward fewer devices per patient.

## 5. How to capitalise, honestly

- **If you believe the volume thesis, the cleanest exposure is Lilly**, which keeps the pen, fill and drug margin, is winning share, and owns the pill that removes devices entirely. Novo is the weaker originator (2026 sales guided 0% to -6%).
- **The best supplier to own is West**, but only after a pullback. It is the one component with a durable regulatory lock-in and visible pricing power.
- **Cartridges are the format winner inside the supply chain.** Schott Pharma and Nipro benefit as the US moves from single-dose autoinjectors to four-dose pens; buy them on weakness, not after an 87% rally.
- **Avoid the capacity layer** (assembly automation, syringes, generic pens, pen needles). It is where the post-shortage overbuild lands, as it did after COVID: West fell 56% and Stevanato 44% from their post-COVID peaks.
