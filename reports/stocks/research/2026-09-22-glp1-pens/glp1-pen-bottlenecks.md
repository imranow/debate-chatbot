# GLP-1 Injection Pens: Where the Bottleneck Is, and Whether It Pays

*Research date 22 September 2026, revised the same day after a QA review (`raw/06-qa-review.md`) and a check of that QA (`raw/07-meta-qa-review.md`). Four research streams (primary containers and needles; pens and autoinjectors; fill-finish, equipment and peptide API; demand and device count) plus a devil's-advocate review. Raw reports with sources are in `raw/`. All figures come from web-search snippets or our own arithmetic on them (marked as estimates), because finance sites are blocked from this sandbox. Verify against filings before acting. Not investment advice.*

---

## 1. The answer first

The thesis is right that far more people will be on GLP-1 drugs. US obesity prescriptions were up 78% year on year in Q2 2026, and the demand stream estimates about 31 million treated patient-years in 2026 rising to about 70 million by 2030. It is wrong that this makes pen suppliers a clean way to own it. Three facts get in the way.

1. **The bottleneck has already moved.** In 2023 to 2024 the binding constraint was sterile fill-finish and pen assembly. FDA declared the tirzepatide shortage resolved in October 2024 (reaffirmed December 2024) and semaglutide in February 2025, as existing lines ramped. Lilly and Novo are now locking in the next wave in-house: Novo bought three Catalent fill sites for $11bn (capacity from 2026), and Lilly has committed more than $50bn to US plants since 2020, including API sites and a device plant due in 2031. The scarce layer in 2026 is peptide drug substance and outsourced fill-finish slots for generics and biotechs, not originator pens.
2. **The winning formats need fewer devices per patient.** Pills (the Wegovy pill is already about 35% of Wegovy prescriptions), four-dose pens (13 devices a year instead of 52) and monthly shots (Pfizer and Amgen in Phase 3) all cut units. The Medicare $50 Bridge covers Wegovy (injection or pill) and Foundayo, but for Zepbound only the four-dose KwikPen, not the single-dose pen or vial. The demand model has delivery units per patient-year falling 28% by 2030 in the base case. In the skeptic's assumed worst case, 2030 autoinjector volume ends up 36% below today even as patients grow.
3. **Most of the value is captive.** Lilly and Novo design and assemble their own pens and own their fill lines. By our rough estimate, the listed suppliers we could itemise earn about $1.1 to 1.5bn a year from GLP-1 work (the top end includes company targets). That is roughly 1 to 1.5% of annualised incretin sales of about $95bn: Lilly's Mounjaro and Zepbound run at about $59bn, and Novo's semaglutide figure of about $35bn is our estimate. Indicative only.

**Where a real moat remains:** coated rubber plungers and seals (West), cartridges for the four-dose pens (Schott, Stevanato, Nipro), and outsourced autoinjector platforms for the next wave of drugs from companies without their own device (Ypsomed, private SHL). Of these, only West combines a durable moat with evidence of pricing power, and at about 42 times earnings it is priced for perfection.

## 2. How a GLP-1 pen is made, and who makes each part

## 3. The device-count reality check

All figures are estimates from the demand stream and the devil's advocate, derived from revenue at assumed net prices.

| | 2026 | 2030 base | 2030 skeptic's worst case |
|---|---|---|---|
| Treated patient-years (estimate) | 31M | 70M | 50M |
| Share oral | 8% | 25% | 40% |
| Autoinjectors and prefilled syringes (weekly single-dose plus monthly) | 463M | 753M | 296M |
| Pens / cartridges (four-dose weekly pens plus daily liraglutide pens)¹ | 271M | 475M | 280M |
| Vials | 82M | 107M | about 57M |
| Pen needles (weekly and daily pens) | 1.49bn | 2.19bn | 1.22bn |
| Delivery units per patient-year (autoinjectors + pens + vials; excludes needles and loose syringes) | 26.5 | 19.1 | about 12.7 |

¹ Four-dose pens alone: 191M / 419M / 260M.

First principles: each million patients who move from a weekly single-dose autoinjector to a four-dose pen removes 52 million autoinjectors and adds 13 million pens (39 million fewer devices in net), plus 52 million pen needles. Each million who move to a monthly shot removes 40 million devices. Each million who move to a pill removes all of them. Supplier revenue therefore tracks the format mix at least as much as patient count. Most US pricing and channel signals (KwikPen price parity, the coming multi-dose Wegovy FlexTouch, pills) point toward fewer devices per patient.

## 5. How to capitalise, honestly

- **If you believe the volume thesis, Lilly captures more of it than any supplier.** It keeps the pen, fill and drug margin, is winning share, and owns the pill that removes devices entirely. Novo is the weaker originator (2026 sales guided 0% to -6%). We have not assessed Lilly's valuation in this work; treat this as a direction for further work, not a recommendation.
- **The best supplier to own is West**, but only after a pullback. It is the one component with a durable regulatory lock-in and visible pricing power.
- **Cartridges are the format winner inside the supply chain, but the listed names are mixed.** Schott Pharma gains from cartridges, but it also sells the 1 mL glass syringes that lose from the same shift, and its FY26 guidance raise cited syringe demand. Nipro makes cartridges inside a much larger, diversified medical group; its packaging data are missing. Buy either only on weakness, not after an 87% rally.
- **Avoid the capacity layer** (assembly automation, syringes, generic pens, pen needles). It is where the post-shortage overbuild lands, as it did after COVID: West fell 56% from its 2021 peak to its July 2025 low, and Stevanato is still 44% below its August 2023 peak (at the post-Q2 price of $19.67).

Price levels are illustrative multiples, not price targets. Not investment advice.
