# What the Big Banks Think About Stocks (October 2026)

*Research date 7 October 2026. Five research streams: strategist targets for ten banks, flagship pick lists and single-stock ratings, a Goldman Sachs deep dive, a JPMorgan deep dive, and Morgan Stanley, Bank of America, UBS and Citi themes. Raw reports with sources are in `raw/`. Every bank document was blocked from this sandbox. So every figure comes from news and aggregator snippets about the research, not from the research itself. Treat all of it as unverified until checked on a terminal or the bank's own portal. Not investment advice.*

---

## 1. The answer first

1. **The banks are bullish, but they have run out of room.** Nine of ten big banks expect the S&P 500 to end 2026 between 7,950 and 8,100. The median is 8,000. The index closed at a record 7,818.93 on 6 October, only 2.3% below that median. Bank of America is the lone bear at 7,400.
2. **The bull case is all earnings, and most of it is AI.** Every target raise in 2026 came from higher earnings forecasts, not higher multiples. Goldman says AI drives about half of 2026 S&P earnings growth. All the banks expect growth to slow from about 24–35% in 2026 to 12–17% in 2027.
3. **No bank calls AI a bubble, but the worry has moved to how it is paid for.** JPMorgan expects $4.1 trillion of AI spending to be debt-funded by 2030. Fund managers in Bank of America's September survey named a disorderly rise in bond yields as the top risk. 42% picked hyperscaler capex as the likeliest source of a credit event.
4. **The real split is on interest rates.** The Fed raised rates on 16 September 2026 for the first time since 2023. Bank of America expects two more hikes this year. Citi expects a pause and a cut in June 2027. Morgan Stanley's Mike Wilson says a 10-year yield above 5% would turn the pause into a correction.
5. **Over ten years, every bank that publishes a forecast expects US stocks to lag.** Goldman expects about 6.5% a year from the S&P 500 and JPMorgan 6.7% for US large caps. Both put Europe and emerging markets higher. Bank of America's valuation model implies about −3% a year for the cap-weighted index.

The implied P/E is our arithmetic: year-end target divided by 2027 earnings per share (EPS), except HSBC's and UBS's own figures.

**How a target is built.** Think of it as a recipe with two ingredients. Price = next year's earnings per share × a multiple. JPMorgan's $420 of 2027 EPS × 19.0 gives about 8,000. The earnings part is a forecast. The multiple is a judgement. On $400 of EPS, one point of multiple is worth 400 index points, about 5%. That one judgement is as large as the whole gap between Bank of America and the rest.

**Why the same number hides different views.** Goldman, JPMorgan, Morgan Stanley and Deutsche Bank all sit at 8,000. But Goldman's 2027 EPS is $385 and JPMorgan's and Deutsche's is $420, a $35 gap. So Goldman is paying a higher multiple for lower earnings.

**How much to trust these targets.** Not much, by the record. Since 2000, year-end strategist targets have missed by about 13–18 percentage points a year on average. A naive rule of "+9% every year" did about as well (Motley Fool using Birinyi data; Jeff Sommer in the NYT; IFA for 2018–23; all snippet-level). The reasons are structural:
- Multiples swing more than earnings, and nobody forecasts them well.
- Career risk rewards staying near the pack. This year nine of ten sit in a 150-point band.
- The consensus has not forecast a down year since 2000 (Bespoke, unverified), so it misses bear markets.
- In bull markets they chase the index. JPMorgan changed its target four times in 2026: a cut to 7,200, then raises to 7,600, 7,800 and 8,000.

## 3. What they agree on

- **Earnings, not multiples, drive the market.** Second-quarter 2026 beat rates were 80–86%, against a long-run average of about 67–73%. Underlying earnings growth was about 35%.
- **AI capex is the engine.** Barclays expects hyperscaler capital spending above $1.1 trillion in 2027. Goldman expects about $800bn in 2026, $1.2 trillion in 2027 and $1.4 trillion in 2028. JPMorgan expects $5.5 trillion in total through 2030, up from $5.1 trillion.
- **The AI tailwind fades.** Goldman says AI capex added about 11 points to 2026 EPS growth, falling to about 7 in 2027 and to zero or below in 2028 as depreciation catches up (unverified, aggregator). Depreciation is the cost of the equipment spread over its life. Once spending stops growing fast, that cost catches up with the revenue boost.
- **Power is the bottleneck, not money.** Goldman sees data-centre power demand 220% higher in 2030 than in 2023. JPMorgan expects $5.8 trillion of grid spending to 2035. Morgan Stanley estimates a US data-centre power gap of about 38 GW through 2028 (later write-ups say 32 GW or 57 GW; conflicting).
- **Inflation, the Fed and bond yields are the shared top risk.** Bank of America, Barclays, Wells Fargo, Morgan Stanley and HSBC all name some version of it.
- **The rally is narrow.** Fewer than half of S&P 500 stocks trade above their 200-day average (Wilson, unverified). Bank of America prefers the equal-weight index to the cap-weighted one.

## 5. The warnings worth hearing

- **Bank of America's fund-manager survey (4–10 September).** Cash rose to 3.9% but stayed under 4%, so its "cash rule" sell signal stays on. The most crowded trade was long semiconductors at 53%, down from a record 82% in July. The 53% matches August's figure, so the two surveys may have been mixed up (unverified).
- **Michael Hartnett (Bank of America).** His Bull & Bear indicator sits around 9.5–9.7, in sell territory. A 3 October summary says stay risk-off until the dollar peaks, and start adding bonds. He sees a Democratic midterm sweep on 3 November as a risk of a 10%+ selloff. All of this comes from secondary write-ups.
- **Mike Wilson (Morgan Stanley).** He warned in mid-September of a correction within 30 days if oil reached $120–140, and later put drawdown risk at 5–10% tied to rising yields.
- **Jamie Dimon (JPMorgan).** He has been the house sceptic all year. He compared markets to 2005–07 in February, saw "too much exuberance" in May, and said "you may have seen peak private credit" in July. In early October he reportedly said AI spending now competes with government borrowing for capital (unverified).
- **UBS's wealth CIO is the main counterweight.** Mark Haefele (17 September) says AI spending is paying off and the rally can run another 6–12 months.

## 8. How to use bank research

- **Watch the changes, not the levels.** Upgrades, downgrades and list additions carry information. A Buy rating on its own carries little. Most published ratings are Buy or Hold, and a Neutral from a big bank is often a polite negative.
- **Targets chase prices.** Bank of America's Hims target moved $32 → $30 → $25 → $36 → $37 within months. JPMorgan's Tesla target went $145 → $475 → $445 → $415. A target is closer to a lagging summary of the price than a forecast.
- **The record is weak.** In one large US study (Bradshaw and Brown, 2005, about 100,000 targets), the price met the 12-month target at the deadline only about 26% of the time (snippet-level).
- **Read the inputs.** Morgan Stanley's "$60 a share for Optimus at 50% odds" is more useful than its $415 Tesla target, because you can disagree with a specific number.
- **Remember the conflicts.** Banks earn fees from the companies they cover. JPMorgan arranged Hims' $400m working-capital facility, and Goldman is a lender to it.
