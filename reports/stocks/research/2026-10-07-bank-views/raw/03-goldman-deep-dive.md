## (a) What Goldman thinks now: 10 bullets

1. AI is not an earnings bubble. Forward P/E is about 19x, near its 10-year average (Snider, Sep 2026).
2. The AI capex boost to S&P 500 earnings peaks in 2026. Capex growth slows from about 94% (2026) to 54% (2027) and 12% (2028); depreciation offsets it by 2028.
3. Within AI, Goldman prefers selectivity and Phase 3 (AI revenue, mostly software) over richly valued Phase 2 infrastructure (Mar 2026). In July it added non-AI, low-correlation themes: consumer experience, compounders, M&A targets.
4. Power demand keeps getting revised up: data-center power +220% by 2030 vs 2023; US data-center load 66 GW by 2027. Delivery delays (only 50-60% on time) are the binding constraint.
5. Humanoid robots: 890k units in 2030 and about 6.5M in 2035, a roughly $138bn market. Japanese autos and components (JTEKT, Harmonic Drive, MinebeaMitsumi, Renesas) are named beneficiaries (unverified list).
6. Obesity drugs: $114bn by 2030, with 40% from pills and $48bn ex-US. Lower price, higher volume. Lilly and Novo keep 82% share.
7. The Fed is in a short hiking cycle. Goldman thinks markets over-price further hikes and sees cuts to about 3.25-3.50% by end-2027.
8. The 10-year should end 2026 near 4.40%, below spot (about 4.80%) and below peers, but term premia stay high (AI borrowing, deficits, energy).
9. China: prefer onshore A-shares (Overweight, CSI 300 5,500) over H-shares (Market Weight). EM is cheap (about 40% discount to US).
10. Long run: the S&P 500 returns about 6.5% a year for 10 years, the lowest of any major region; EM about 10.9%. Diversify away from the US.
