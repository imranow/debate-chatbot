## Morgan Stanley

### 1. AI capex and power
- Power gap, August 2026: US data centres need about 68 GW in 2026-2028. About 15 GW is under construction. About 15 GW is available or contracted grid capacity. That leaves about a 38 GW shortfall. Gas turbines could supply 15-20 GW of it through 2028. (Motley Fool / Yahoo Finance, 20 Aug 2026.)
- Same date: Morgan Stanley says the data-centre "big money era" is over. "Capital alone no longer clears a site." Power access, not money, is the binding constraint. (Fortune, 20 Aug 2026.)
- Later revisions (conflicting, secondary only):
  - Gap raised to about 57 GW: 97 GW demand, less 21 GW under construction, less 19 GW grid. Phasing about 5 GW (2026), 12 GW (2027), 33 GW (2028). The value of powering a site a year early put at $4.50 per watt. (BigGo Finance, about late Sept 2026.) (unverified)
  - About 32 GW through 2028 even after on-site generation and fuel cells, or 34% of the new power needed. (Igor's Lab, about 5 Oct 2026.) (unverified)
  - A probability-weighted net gap of only 1-11 GW. (X post, Milk Road AI.) (unverified, social media)
- Financing: "Bridging a $1.5tr Data Center Financing Gap". About $2.9tr global data-centre capex in 2025-2028. Hyperscaler cash flow covers about $1.4tr. About $800bn from private credit, $200bn corporate bonds, $150bn securitised. (Morgan Stanley Research PDF, 2025. Older: flagged.)
- MS also estimated AI capex could add about $1tr of big-tech debt by 2028. (Futu / Longbridge; date not confirmed.) (unverified)
- Favoured beneficiaries (theme level): power equipment, gas turbines, grid and industrials that "fill the gap". (Motley Fool, 20 Aug 2026.) Stock names excluded per scope.
- AI bubble view: no explicit 2026 MS "bubble" call found. Its framing is that power and liquidity, not demand, are the constraints.

### 2. Physical AI / humanoids
- "The Humanoid 100" (Feb 2025; Older: flagged): maps 100 listed firms across Brain (AI, chips, software), Body (sensors, actuators, batteries) and Integrators. Framed against a $60tr TAM. (Investing.com; MS advisor PDF.)
- Long-run model: about 1bn humanoids by 2050. About 24.4m adopted by 2036. Annual revenue up to about $7.5tr by 2050 in one write-up; about $5tr in another. US alone about $3tr by 2050. High-income ASP from $200,000 (2024) to about $75,000 (2050). (Robotics & Automation News, 2 May 2025; robozaps blog. Older: flagged.) The $5tr vs $7.5tr figures conflict.
- China 2026 shipments: forecast raised to 50,000 units in June 2026. It was 28,000, which was itself doubled from 14,000 in January. China market $2bn in 2026 and $15bn by 2030. 446,000 units in 2030. Driver: Xpeng and others plan mass production by year-end. (CNBC, 24 June 2026; SCMP. Slightly older than July: flagged.)
- MS expected at least one big tech firm or AI lab to announce formal robotics plans in 2026. (Tiger Brokers / BigGo, early 2026. Older: flagged.)
- No July-October 2026 MS humanoid unit update found.

## Bank of America

### 1. AI (Hartnett and the survey)
- Hartnett's frame (Flow Show "Let Them Eat Data", 3 Sept 2026): policymakers know rising long yields are the biggest threat to the AI capex boom. Central banks and governments are increasingly willing to stop yields rising. ("Policymakers are trying to cap yields and keep the bubble alive", Kodiak Trades Substack; Finvaulta summary.) (unverified: secondary)
- The mix he favours: duration (bonds), debasement hedges (gold), international equities over US megacaps. (Finvaulta summary, Sept 2026.) (unverified)
- Key invalidation test: if long yields rise sharply despite policy, pressure hits long-duration assets: long Treasuries, REITs, high-duration AI names. (Finvaulta summary.) (unverified)
- 3 Oct 2026 Flow Show "A Tail of Two Cities": stay risk-off until the dollar peaks; start adding bonds; rising yields weaken the broad market and credit stress threatens the AI boom. (GoldFix Substack "Hartnett says start adding bonds", about 3 Oct 2026.) (unverified: secondary)

## Where these four banks agree and disagree

### Agree
- AI capex is still rising and is the main earnings driver. None of the four calls a bubble outright. UBS and Citi Wealth are the most explicit that the spend is paying off.
- Power is the bottleneck. MS quantifies a 32-57 GW gap; UBS has "Power and resources" as a core theme.
- Bond yields, not AI valuations, are the near-term threat. MS (Wilson), BofA (Hartnett and the FMS tail risk) and UBS (24 and 29 Sept notes) all centre on long yields.
- No recession in the base case. BofA says strong nominal growth lowers recession risk. The FMS has only 2% expecting a hard landing. MS ties recession only to an extreme oil shock.
- Humanoids are a multi-decade, very large market: MS about 1bn units by 2050; Citi about 650m by 2050; BofA 3bn installed by 2060.

### Disagree

| Near-term stance | Correction risk (5-10%), buy quality | Risk-off until dollar peaks; add bonds (Hartnett) | Stay invested; rally continues 6-12m | Boom, but watch capex cracks |

- The biggest split is the Fed: BofA is the most hawkish (two more hikes). Citi is the most dovish (pause, cut mid-2027).
- On sentiment, BofA's own gauges (FMS cash rule, Bull & Bear) say "sell". UBS CIO says stay invested. MS sits between: correction, not a bear market.
