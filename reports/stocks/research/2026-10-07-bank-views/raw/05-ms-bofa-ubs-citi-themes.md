# Morgan Stanley, BofA, UBS, Citi: themes, cross-asset calls, contrarian warnings

As of 7 October 2026. Work in progress. Sections fill in as research proceeds.

Scope: themes, macro and cross-asset, positioning, warnings. Excludes S&P targets, EPS, sector weights, single-stock picks, Goldman and JPMorgan.

## Morgan Stanley

### AI capex and power
- Power gap, August 2026: US data centres need about 68 GW in 2026-2028. About 15 GW is under construction and 15 GW is available or contracted grid capacity. That leaves about a 38 GW shortfall. Gas turbines could supply 15-20 GW through 2028. (Motley Fool / Yahoo, 20 Aug 2026; Fortune, 20 Aug 2026.)
- Fortune, 20 Aug 2026: Morgan Stanley says the "big money era" of data centres is over: "Capital alone no longer clears a site". Power access, not capital, is the binding constraint.
- Later revisions (conflicting figures, secondary only): one report says the gap was raised to 57 GW (97 GW demand, less 21 GW under construction, less 19 GW grid), phased about 5 GW in 2026, 12 GW in 2027, 33 GW in 2028. Value of powering a site a year early put at $4.50 per watt. (BigGo Finance, about late Sept 2026.) (unverified)
- Another write-up dated about 5 Oct 2026 says the gap is about 32 GW through 2028 even after on-site generation and fuel cells, or 34% of new power needed. (Igor's Lab.) (unverified)
- A social-media post describes a probability-weighted net shortfall of only 1-11 GW. (X, Milk Road AI.) (unverified, social media)
- Financing: "Bridging a $1.5tr Data Center Financing Gap". About $2.9tr global data-centre capex 2025-2028. Hyperscaler cash flow covers about $1.4tr. About $800bn from private credit, $200bn corporate bonds, $150bn securitised. (Morgan Stanley Research PDF; 2025 report, older than July 2026: flagged.)
- Morgan Stanley also estimated AI capex could add about $1tr of tech-giant debt by 2028. (Futu / Longbridge; date not confirmed.) (unverified)

### Physical AI / humanoids
- "The Humanoid 100" (Feb 2025, equity research; older: flagged) maps 100 listed firms across Brain (AI, chips, software), Body (sensors, actuators, batteries) and Integrators. Framed against a $60tr TAM. (Investing.com; MS advisor PDF.)
- Long-run model: about 1bn humanoids by 2050; about $7.5tr annual revenue by 2050. About $5tr market by 2050 in other coverage. US alone about $3tr by 2050. (Robotics & Automation News, 2 May 2025; robozaps blog.) Older: flagged.
- China 2026 shipments: forecast raised to 50,000 units in June 2026, from 28,000, which itself was doubled from 14,000 in January. China market $2bn in 2026, $15bn by 2030; 446,000 units in 2030. Catalyst: Xpeng and others plan mass production by year-end. (CNBC, 24 June 2026; SCMP.) Slightly older than July: flagged.
- Expected at least one major tech firm or AI lab to formally announce robotics plans in 2026. (Tiger Brokers; BigGo, early 2026.) Older: flagged.
- No newer (July-Oct 2026) MS unit forecast found.

### Macro
- Mid-year 2026 outlook (Seth Carpenter, chief global economist): Fed holds for the rest of 2026; maybe two cuts in H1 2027 if inflation falls. (Morgan Stanley Insights; mid-2026.) Note: the Fed in fact hiked 25bp in September 2026 (per UBS CIO, 24 Sept 2026). So the MS mid-year call appears overtaken. Latest MS Fed call after the hike: not found.
- Recession risk is tied to oil. If oil disruption persists, oil could go through $150 and become a "volume shock", which could mean recession. (Carpenter, MS podcast, 2026; date not confirmed.)
- Dollar: Carpenter expects higher policy rates and a stronger dollar than markets assumed; FX team favours a stronger dollar, especially versus the yen. (MS podcast, 2026; date not confirmed.) (unverified)
- Tariffs: no fresh MS tariff call found.

## Bank of America

### Positioning: Global Fund Manager Survey (September 2026, latest found)
- Survey window 4-10 Sept 2026; published 15 Sept 2026. About 170 global respondents with $470bn AUM (some write-ups say 190 incl. regional). Source: secondary write-ups only (ChainCatcher, CryptoBriefing, Finvaulta, Webull/Futu). BofA primary not accessed.
- Cash rose to 3.9% from 3.5% in August. Largest monthly rise since March 2026. Still below BofA's 4.0% threshold, so the contrarian "cash rule" sell signal stays active. (CryptoBriefing, 19 Sept 2026; Bitget.)
- Most crowded trade: long global semiconductors, 53%. Fourth straight month at the top. Down from a record 82% in July. (ChainCatcher, 20 Sept 2026.)
- Second most crowded: short government bonds, 18%, first time in second place. Long Magnificent 7 fell to 7%. (ChainCatcher, 20 Sept 2026.) (unverified: single secondary source)
- Biggest tail risk: disorderly rise in bond yields, 33%. It overtook "AI bubble". (Finvaulta summary, 15 Sept 2026; Webull.)
- 42% name hyperscaler capex as the most likely source of a systemic credit event, up from 38% in August. (CryptoBriefing; Webull.)
- A record share (33%) say companies are over-investing. (ChainCatcher.) (unverified)
- Global equity net overweight fell to 49% from 56%. Composite sentiment fell to 7.0 from 8.0. (Bitget; CryptoBriefing.)
- Bonds: net 48% underweight, lowest since May 2022. (Finvaulta.) (unverified)
- Macro: 55% expect "no landing", 38% soft landing, 2% hard landing. (Webull.) One outlet (Futu) mislabels the 55% as soft landing: conflicting.
- Fed: 41% think the Fed may hike before the November midterms, up from 22% in August. (Webull/Futu.)
- European FMS (10 Sept 2026): 43% see AI investment disappointment as the biggest downside risk to global growth, up from 21%. (Finvaulta.) (unverified)
- Earlier context: July 2026 FMS cash fell to 3.6% from 4.1%, triggering the sell signal; 82% called long semis most crowded, a record ("most crowded trade ever", 24/7 Wall St, 15 July 2026). August 2026: cash 3.5%, semis 53%, short yen 12%, long Mag 7 11%. (Livewire; investinglive.)
- July 2026 FMS per an X post (Stock Talk): record 54% "no landing"; 61% did not expect hyperscalers to cut capex; year-end oil forecast $71, down from $86 in June. (unverified, social media)
- October 2026 FMS: not yet published as of 7 Oct (due mid-October).

## UBS (incl. GWM CIO)

### AI
- 17 Sept 2026, House View Monthly Letter (Mark Haefele, CIO GWM): equity rally should continue over 6-12 months. Q2 results show AI infrastructure spending is paying off in demand and returns. Risks: tight memory supply and a debate over AI safety. (UBS PDF.)
- UBS CIO projects global AI capex rising from about $900bn in 2026 to $1.2tr in 2027. (UBS CIO weekly, 2026.) (unverified: date of the note not confirmed)
- Late Sept 2026: AI investment should keep supporting earnings. (UBS CIO daily.) EPS numbers excluded per scope.
- Favoured AI exposure: diversified across infrastructure, networking, software, applications and AI "adopters". Concentrated AI or megacap holdings can be a source of funds for broader exposure. ("Strategic: Invest in transformational innovation", about mid-Sept 2026.)
- Bubble view: Year Ahead 2026 (Nov 2025) says capex and adoption fuel further gains but "be mindful of bubble risks". Older: flagged.

### Power, longevity, defence, humanoids
- Transformational innovation themes: AI, Power and resources, Longevity. (UBS CIO, mid-Sept 2026.)
- Power and resources: benefits from rising electricity use and grid and data-centre investment. Likes the electrification value chain. (UBS CIO.)
- Longevity: market from $5.3tr in 2023 to $8.0tr by 2030; health care about a quarter. (UBS CIO.) Covers obesity drugs only indirectly; a specific UBS CIO GLP-1 forecast: not found.
- Defence: not a named CIO theme. A UBS markets article (about May 2026; older: flagged) says rising spending boosts demand for munitions, missiles, drones, radars and warships.
- Humanoids: no UBS forecast found.

### Macro and cross-asset
- Fed: Fed and ECB both hiked 25bp in September 2026. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh said inflation is "too high". UBS expects one more Fed hike in December, then a hold. It expects less tightening than markets price. (UBS CIO daily, 24 Sept 2026: "Higher yields need not derail markets".)
- 4 Sept 2026 daily ("What a more hawkish Fed means for investors"): higher real rates and a stronger dollar may be near-term headwinds for gold. Gold remains a hedge. Trim excess dollar holdings into strength. (UBS CIO.)
- 29 Sept 2026: long-dated Treasury yields at multi-decade highs. UBS still sees resilient growth and earnings absorbing them. (UBS CIO.)
- Recession odds: no explicit UBS figure found.
- Tariffs: no fresh UBS CIO call found.


## Citi

### AI
- Citi strategists call the market a "boom" not a bubble, but warn an AI slowdown could trigger earnings downgrades and hit 2026 gains. (CryptoBriefing; date about 2026, not confirmed.) (unverified)
- Citi Wealth: fears of weaker AI spending are "overblown"; S&P 500 2026 capex expectations rose more than 5% in three months. ("AI Capex Keeps Rising as Investors Debate Returns", Citi Wealth weekly; date not confirmed.)
- Citi Wealth Q2 2026 "Short and Long": cracks in AI spending could pressure the market; capex beneficiaries may de-rate. (Q2 2026; older: flagged.)

### Physical AI / humanoids
- Citi GPS "The Rise of AI Robots: Physical AI is Coming for You" (Dec 2024; older: flagged): 1.3bn AI robots by 2035 and 4bn by 2050. About 648m-650m humanoids by 2050; market about $7tr by 2050. A $15,000 robot breaks even in 3.8 weeks versus a $41/hour job. (Citi GPS; CNBC 23 Feb 2026.)
- 2026 Citi AI Summit: general-purpose home humanoids will take longer than hoped; faster progress in warehouses and factories. (Citi Ventures, 2026.)
- Citi survey of Chinese suppliers (late 2025): global output to at least double in 2026. (unverified; older: flagged)
