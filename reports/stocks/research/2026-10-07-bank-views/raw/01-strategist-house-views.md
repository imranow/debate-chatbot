## Method and caveats
- Source: 18 web searches. Every finance and news site I tried to open directly was blocked (Yahoo Finance, CNBC, Quartz, morganstanley.com, FRED). So every figure here comes from search-result snippets and the search tool's summaries of them. None comes from reading a full article.
- So every figure is snippet-level. "(unverified)" marks a claim that has only one source, or that came only from an aggregator or crypto-news site, or that conflicts with another source. Figures with no tag were seen in at least two separate outlets' headlines or snippets.
- "not found" means I searched and found nothing. I have not guessed.
- Freshness: four banks have views dated September 2026 (Barclays, HSBC, BofA, plus Goldman's 12-month call). Four last moved in June to August 2026 (JPM, UBS, MS, Wells Fargo). Two have older figures: Citi (around June 2026) and Deutsche Bank (its target dates from its late-2025 outlook; it raised EPS in August 2026). I found no October 2026 target change at any bank.

### 1. Goldman Sachs: Ben Snider (chief US equity strategist)
- **Target:** On 26–27 May 2026 Goldman raised its YE2026 target to **8,000** from 7,600 (Bloomberg, 27 May 2026; Yahoo Finance). In mid-September 2026 it gave a **12-month target of about 8,700**, roughly 14% upside (unverified: BigGo Finance aggregator, plus a 24/7 Wall St piece dated 13 Sep 2026). - **EPS:** **$340 for 2026** (+24% y/y) and **$385 for 2027** (+13%), raised in May. In March 2026 the figures were about $309 and $342. In December 2025 they were $305 and $336 (Newsquawk, 12 Dec 2025).
- **Implied P/E:** The bank says the forward multiple stays "close to the current 21". My check: 8,000 / $385 = 20.8x 2027 EPS.
- **Why it raised:** Earnings. AI-infrastructure beneficiaries drive about half of 2026 index EPS growth.
- **Sectors and themes:** The most attractive AI plays are AI infrastructure (semis, servers, networking), power infrastructure, and hyperscalers. Goldman notes hyperscaler P/Es sit near the low end of their 10-year range. I found no formal overweight or underweight list.
- **AI bubble view:** Goldman calls bubble fears overdone. It expects profit growth to slow, not collapse. The P/E is lower than a year ago even though the index is up more than 20%. AI capex added about 11pp to 2026 EPS growth. Goldman sees that falling to about 7pp in 2027 and turning slightly negative in 2028 (unverified: aggregator, September 2026).
- **Style and size:** Goldman Research makes a case for small-cap outperformance. It cites roughly 12% average small-cap outperformance after past Fed cutting cycles, though the Fed is now hiking. The date of that note is unclear (unverified).
- **Risks:** Semiconductor margins could fall back toward their historical ~55% average. That would cut about 10% from S&P EPS (unverified). Concentration: the top 10 stocks make up 41% of market cap and 34% of earnings (older figure). A near-term pullback on technicals.

## Where they agree
1. **Earnings drive the market, not multiples.** Every 2026 raise I found was driven by EPS. Q2 2026 beats were exceptional: 80–86% beat rates, and about 35% underlying growth.
2. **AI capex is the core earnings engine.** Goldman says AI is about half of 2026 EPS growth. Barclays sees hyperscaler capex above $1.1trn in 2027. Wells Fargo and HSBC point to semis and "CapEx takers".
3. **No bank calls AI a bubble outright.** Goldman explicitly rejects the comparison. BofA's caution centres on valuation, inflation and credit, not an AI collapse.
4. **The 2027 slowdown is expected.** EPS growth falls from about 24–35% in 2026 to about 12–17% in 2027 across banks.
5. **Inflation and rates are the shared top risk.** BofA, Barclays, Wells Fargo, Morgan Stanley and HSBC all name inflation, the Fed or yields. This came before and after the 16 Sep hike.
6. **Narrow breadth and concentration are acknowledged.** Morgan Stanley's breadth gap, BofA's equal-weight preference, HSBC's megacap note and Goldman's top-10 weight all point the same way.

## Where they disagree most
1. **Level: BofA against everyone else.** BofA's 7,400 YE target sits below the current index level (about −5%). The other nine are at or above it. BofA also expects about −3% a year on the cap-weighted index over 10 years.
2. **Cap-weight vs equal-weight and size:** BofA prefers equal-weight, value and SMID. Morgan Stanley IM is overweight SMID. Goldman makes a small-cap case. Wells Fargo Investment Institute advised against small caps (unverified, date unclear).
3. **The Fed path:** Before 16 Sep, Wilson's base case was a hold, and the Fed hiked. HSBC calls hike fears "overdone". BofA and Barclays stress a hawkish path as a key risk.
4. **Valuation:** Implied multiples range from 19.0x 2027 EPS (JPM, DB) to 22.5x 2026 EPS (HSBC). Banks with the same 8,000 target use EPS that differ by $35 (Goldman $385 vs JPM and DB $420). The same price target hides very different P/E views.
5. **Utilities and power:** Goldman likes power infrastructure. Barclays cut utilities to neutral on wildfire and permitting risk (unverified). MS IM is overweight European utilities.

## How a strategist target is built (first principles)
- **Price = EPS × P/E.** A strategist forecasts next year's index earnings per share. Then they choose a multiple and multiply.
  - Example: JPM's $420 2027 EPS × 19.0x = 7,980 ≈ 8,000.
  - A year-end 2026 target is usually priced on 2027 ("forward") EPS, because at year-end the market looks a year ahead. HSBC is an exception: it quotes 22.5x on 2026 EPS.
- **The EPS part** is built bottom-up from analyst estimates and top-down from GDP, margins, the dollar and capex. In 2026 most of it rides on a few AI names.
- **The P/E part** is a judgement. It is tied to real yields, inflation, the equity risk premium and history. A 1-point change in the multiple on $400 EPS moves the target by 400 points, about 5%. That is as big as the entire gap between BofA and the rest.
- **Why the record is poor:**
  1. Multiples swing far more than EPS, and nobody forecasts them well.
  2. Shocks (wars, Fed pivots, pandemics) are by nature unforecastable.
  3. Career risk rewards herding near consensus. This year nine of ten targets sit in a 150-point band.
  4. Targets almost always point up. Since 2000 the consensus has never forecast a down year (Bespoke; Bloomberg Opinion). So they miss bear markets.
  5. In bull markets they lag and keep chasing. Every bank here except DB raised its target at least once in 2026. JPM revised four times: a cut, then three raises.
- **Evidence on the track record (snippet-level):**
  - Motley Fool, using Birinyi and S&P Capital IQ data: the average miss since 2000 was **14.7 percentage points a year**. A naive "+9% every year" rule missed by 14.1pp. The strategists were no better than a constant. https://www.fool.com/investing/general/2015/02/25/the-blind-forecaster.aspx
  - Jeff Sommer (NYT), cited by Bernoff: the average error since 2000 was **12.9pp**. https://bernoff.com/blog/financial-strategist-market-forecasts-are-worthless-but-not-useless
  - IFA, for 2018–2023: the median estimate missed by about **18pp** on average, ranging from 26% too low to 21% too high. https://www.ifa.com/articles/analysis-market-forecasting-accuracy
  - Bespoke: strategists underestimated the year in 7 of 9 years after 2009, and missed 2017 by about 14pp (via Mutual Fund Observer). https://mutualfundobserver.com/discuss/discussion/37533/bespoke-2018-wall-street-strategists-year-end-price-targets
  - An analysis cited by Quartz/Yahoo in Sep 2026 says strategists underestimated returns in 13 of the past 16 years, by about 10% on average (unverified, single snippet).
  - Welch & Goyal (2008): most return-prediction models do not beat the historical average out of sample (cited in search summary).
