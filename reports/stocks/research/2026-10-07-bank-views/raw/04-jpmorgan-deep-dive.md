# JPMorgan deep dive: thematic and cross-asset views (as of 7 Oct 2026)

Scope: J.P. Morgan Global Research (GR), J.P. Morgan Asset Management (JPMAM) and J.P. Morgan Private Bank (PB), plus Jamie Dimon's public comments.
Excluded (covered in files 01 and 02): S&P 500 targets, EPS forecasts, sector over/underweights, focus lists and single-stock ratings.

## Method and caveats

- 16 web searches. All jpmorgan.com, am.jpmorgan.com and fortune.com fetches were blocked by the sandbox proxy. So every figure below comes from search-engine snippets of JPMorgan pages or from news coverage. No primary PDF was read in full.
- "(unverified)" = one source only, or a secondary/aggregator source.
- "(older)" = dated before July 2026.
- JPMorgan is not one voice. GR (the sell-side), JPMAM (the asset manager) and PB (the wealth arm) often disagree. Each view is labelled by unit.
- Big 2026 backdrop, as JPMorgan describes it: a Middle East conflict and a closure of the Strait of Hormuz. PB called it "the largest oil supply shock since World War II" (11 May 2026). The Fed then raised rates on 16 Sep 2026.

## 1. AI

### AI capex size and financing (GR)
- Global AI-related capex: **$5.5 trillion through 2030**, up from $5.1 trillion. GR mid-year outlook, late June 2026. Source: Fortune, 25 Jun 2026; Bloomberg Law. Two outlets agree.
  - Debt financing estimate raised to **$4.1 trillion**, because loan-to-cost ratios are higher. Loan-to-cost averages above 85%; some deals exceed 90%.
  - AI operating cash flow forecast to pass **$900 billion by 2027**. JPMorgan's case: the build-out is still profitable "for now" (Fortune's headline words).
  - https://fortune.com/2026/06/25/what-bubble-jpmorgan-5-5-trillion-ai-capex-explosion-profitable-for-now/
  - https://news.bloomberglaw.com/texas-brief/jpmorgan-sees-ai-buildout-costs-totaling-5-5-trillion-by-2030
- AI-linked US high-grade bond issuance: **about $266 billion year-to-date** in a 9 Sep 2026 GR credit note. Hyperscalers $182bn, data-centre operators $42bn, semis $42bn. That beats all of 2025 ($139bn) and is more than 9x 2024 ($29bn). The note sees **about $2.1 trillion** of AI capex financing needed from the HG market over five years. Key risk it names: funding is moving from internal cash flow to debt, so AI capital costs now track credit conditions. The write-up's headline: "investment booms rarely end gently". (unverified: one secondary source, TechFlow, which cites a third-party research firm.)
  - https://techflowpost.com/en-US/newsletter/135840
- Data-centre capex growth for 2026 raised to **above 50%**, up from 30%. GR, December 2025. (older; unverified)
  - https://www.itiger.com/news/1145735731

### Bubble risk

- **Michael Cembalest** (Chairman of Market and Investment Strategy, JPMAM/PB), "Smothering Heights", Eye on the Market 2026 outlook, January 2026 (older). Four medium-term risks: US power-generation limits; China building its own tech moat; Taiwan; and a **"metaverse moment" for hyperscaler profits** after $1.3 trillion of capex and R&D since late 2022. He notes 42 AI-linked firms made 65–75% of S&P 500 earnings, revenue and capex since Q4 2022.
  - https://privatebank.jpmorgan.com/nam/en/insights/latest-and-featured/eotm/outlook
- Cembalest, 22 Jul 2026: turned **"cautious" on US equity technicals**. He flagged that chip stocks surged while hyperscalers stalled. (unverified: secondary summary by Motley Fool, 3 Aug 2026.)
  - https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/08/03/top-jpmorgan-strategist-michael-cembalest-cautious/

US and EM equity markets are "incredibly concentrated" in AI at sector and single-stock level. A commentary (Advisor Analyst, 1 Oct 2026) reports: AI-related industries = **53.2% of S&P 500 market cap**; Magnificent 7 = **35%**; hyperscaler capex projected to rise from **$416bn (2025) to $1.2 trillion (2028)**. (figures unverified: secondary summary; PDF not read.)

## (a) 10-bullet summary: what JPMorgan thinks now

1. AI capex is huge and still profitable: $5.5tn through 2030, $900bn+ operating cash flow by 2027 (GR, Jun 2026).
2. The AI risk has moved from "is it real?" to "how is it funded?": $4.1tn of debt to 2030; record AI bond issuance in 2026 (GR, Jun and Sep 2026).
3. Favour the broader AI chain: semis, equipment, components, power and grid, Asian chip supply chain, over mega-cap hyperscalers (JPMAM mid-year 2026; Cembalest Jul 2026).
4. Diversify away from AI concentration: about 53% of S&P 500 cap is AI-linked (Guide to the Markets 4Q 2026).
5. Power is the binding constraint: $5.8tn grid spend to 2035; power demand +3.6% a year 2026–30; gas stays central.
6. The Fed is hiking, not cutting: one more hike in December after September, then stop (Feroli, 25 Sep 2026). Yields drift higher. The dollar is a buy (GR, Jul 2026).
7. Recession is not the base case: JPMAM puts it at 5% (4Q 2026). GR's last published number was 35% (start of 2026).
8. Regions: EM overweight; India the top idea outside the US; Japan positive but fully valued; China selective (AI and semis self-reliance); Europe a possible H2 catch-up plus defence and electrification.
9. Long run: US large caps 6.7% a year, below Europe (8.5%) and EM (7.2%); 60/40 6.4%; add alternatives for 6.9% (LTCMA 2026, Oct 2025).
10. Dimon stays the house skeptic: too much exuberance, correction risk underpriced, private credit losses coming, and AI capex now competes with government debt for capital.
